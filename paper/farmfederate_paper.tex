\title{FarmFederate: Federated LLM, ViT, and VLM for Privacy-Preserving Crop Stress Detection}

\author{Ayush Debnath, Ruelia Saha, and Sudip Misra}

\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
Accurate crop stress detection is essential for precision agriculture, yet existing approaches rely on a single modality in isolation and almost universally assume centralized data access. This paper presents \textbf{FarmFederate}, a unified framework that integrates four privacy-preserving federated components into a single end-to-end pipeline: \textit{Federated LLM} (distributed text classification via Large Language Model encoders), \textit{Federated ViT} (distributed image classification via Vision Transformer encoders), \textit{Federated VLM} (distributed multimodal fusion via Vision-Language Model architectures), and \textit{Federated RAG} (privacy-preserving Retrieval-Augmented Generation for actionable treatment advisory). Each component trains under FedAvg across $K{=}3$ non-IID clients; the RAG layer further employs a dual-encoder retriever jointly trained with the classifier using InfoNCE and BCE losses, with per-farm FAISS knowledge stores that are \emph{never} transmitted to the server. We benchmark five LLM variants, five ViT variants, eight VLM fusion strategies (Concatenation, Cross-Attention, Gated, CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP-2, CoCa, and Unified-IO), and the integrated RAG advisory pipeline on a 5-class crop stress dataset. Experiments on a Tesla T4 GPU show that Federated LLM achieves F1 of 0.608 (100.8\% of centralized baseline), Federated ViT achieves 0.816 (98.9\%), and Federated VLM achieves 0.839 (97.9\%), with Concatenation fusion reaching the highest VLM F1 of 0.848 and ViT-Base achieving 0.857. The Federated RAG advisory achieves 100\% knowledge-base coverage, perfect classification F1, Recall@5\,=\,0.7327, MRR\,=\,0.800, and NDCG@5\,=\,0.8936 under joint federated training, with MRR and NDCG@5 both exceeding their ${\geq}0.70$ targets. Across all four components, federated training retains over 97\% of centralized performance while guaranteeing that no raw farm data is ever transmitted. A stress-biased evaluation confirms near-perfect F1 (0.989--1.000) under regional distribution shifts. These results establish FarmFederate as a comprehensive and modular framework for privacy-preserving agricultural AI, spanning perception, classification, and actionable advisory generation entirely within the federated paradigm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{P}{recision} agriculture has emerged as a critical domain for advanced machine learning, driven by the need to safeguard global food security~\cite{kamilaris2018deep}. Crop stress detection---encompassing drought, nutrient deficiency, disease, pest infestation, and heat stress---enables timely intervention and yield optimization. Conventional methods typically rely on either visual examination of crop imagery or interpretation of textual symptom reports, but rarely integrate both modalities effectively.

Deep learning has yielded substantial progress in agricultural image classification~\cite{mohanty2016using}. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and, more recently, Vision Transformers (ViTs) have demonstrated strong performance on plant disease datasets. Concurrently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown remarkable capacity for understanding written symptom descriptions~\cite{devlin2018bert}. In practice, however, crop stress classification involves both visual symptoms (leaf discoloration, wilting patterns) and textual information (environmental conditions, growth stage descriptions), motivating multimodal approaches.

Agricultural data is inherently distributed across farms, regions, and institutions. Centralized data aggregation is often infeasible due to privacy concerns, data ownership regulations, and bandwidth limitations. Federated Learning (FL)~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} enables collaborative model training without sharing raw data, making it well-suited for agricultural applications where farms may be unwilling to share proprietary crop data. Crucially, classification alone is insufficient for practical deployment: after a stress category is identified, farmers need concrete, context-specific treatment guidance. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) bridges this gap by retrieving relevant agronomic knowledge and generating natural-language advisories---and can be made fully privacy-preserving by keeping knowledge stores local to each farm under the federated umbrella.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work addresses five research questions: (a)~How does Federated LLM text encoding perform for crop stress classification, and how much accuracy is lost relative to centralized training? (b)~How does Federated ViT image encoding compare, and is it more or less robust to non-IID federated partitioning than text-based approaches? (c)~Does Federated VLM multimodal fusion provide sufficient accuracy gains to justify its higher communication and compute cost? (d)~Which of the three federated classification paradigms---Federated LLM, Federated ViT, or Federated VLM---offers the best privacy-performance trade-off? (e)~Can a Retrieval-Augmented Generation advisory layer be integrated into the federated pipeline without violating privacy guarantees, and what retrieval quality does it achieve under joint federated training? Our specific contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Federated LLM:} A federated text classification paradigm using five lightweight LLM encoder variants (DistilBERT, BERT-tiny, RoBERTa-tiny, ALBERT-tiny, MobileBERT), trained independently under FedAvg and benchmarked against centralized baselines on real HuggingFace agricultural corpora.

    \item \textbf{Federated ViT:} A federated image classification paradigm using five Vision Transformer-inspired encoder variants (ViT-Base, DeiT-tiny, Swin-tiny, ConvNeXT-tiny, EfficientNet), trained independently under FedAvg and evaluated on synthetic crop stress imagery with tuned class-discriminative difficulty.

    \item \textbf{Federated VLM:} A federated multimodal fusion paradigm implementing eight VLM fusion strategies (Concatenation, Cross-Attention, Gated, CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP-2, CoCa, Unified-IO), trained independently under FedAvg and compared against both unimodal federated paradigms and centralized VLM baselines.

    \item \textbf{Cross-Paradigm Comparison:} A systematic empirical comparison of all three federated paradigms---Federated LLM, Federated ViT, and Federated VLM---quantifying the accuracy, federated efficiency (Fed/Cent ratio), and prediction diversity of each, providing actionable guidance for practitioners selecting a federated agricultural AI paradigm.

    \item A joint optimization formulation with focal loss and diversity regularization that prevents class collapse across all three federated paradigms, and a stress-biased evaluation protocol that tests model robustness under simulated regional distribution shifts.

    \item \textbf{Federated RAG Advisory:} A privacy-preserving Retrieval-Augmented Generation layer integrated as the fourth pipeline stage. A \texttt{RAGQueryBuilder} projects the fused embedding $\mathbf{h}_f$, class probabilities, and IoT sensor readings into a 128-d query; a dual-encoder retriever (DistilRoBERTa backbone) trained jointly with the federated classifier via InfoNCE and BCE losses retrieves from per-farm FAISS knowledge stores; and an LLM generates natural-language treatment advisories. Farm-side stores are \emph{never} transmitted to the server. The retriever is stabilised across rounds via EMA aggregation ($\mu{=}0.9$).

    \item \textbf{Integrated End-to-End Evaluation:} A comprehensive evaluation covering all four federated components under identical non-IID conditions ($\alpha{=}0.5$ Dirichlet), with a cross-paradigm comparison table, stress-biased evaluation protocol, and analysis of RAG retrieval quality (Recall@5, MRR, NDCG@5, KB Coverage, embedding drift).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Plant disease detection.} CNN-based approaches pioneered on PlantVillage achieve 99\% accuracy~\cite{mohanty2016using,ferentinos2018deep}. Vision transformers (Swin, DeiT) now match or exceed CNN performance~\cite{fahimulislam2024wheat,zhang2025swin}, and multimodal UAV pipelines combining YOLO detection with DeiT classification achieve 99.45\%~\cite{alobeidat2025multimodal}. All these approaches assume centralized data, precluding privacy-sensitive farm deployments.

\textbf{Federated learning for agriculture.} FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} has been applied with CNNs to soybean (93\%)~\cite{kumar2024soybean}, rice (99\%)~\cite{aggarwal2023rice}, mango~\cite{mehta2024mango}, banana~\cite{sharma2024banana}, and other crops. Kabala~et~al.~\cite{kabala2023image} compared CNNs and ViTs under FL; Aldossary~et~al.~\cite{aldossary2025levit} combined LeViT with ResUNet for drone+IoT monitoring (98.9\%). Surveys confirm data heterogeneity and bandwidth as key FL challenges for agriculture~\cite{kondaveeti2024fl,belghachi2025fl,puppala2026satellite}. Praharaj~et~al.~\cite{praharaj2025adversarial} studied adversarial threats in federated smart farming.

\textbf{VLMs and LLMs for agriculture.} CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning}, BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip}, Flamingo~\cite{alayrac2022flamingo}, and CoCa~\cite{yu2022coca} provide the VLM architectures we adapt. AgriHealth-LLM~\cite{long2025agrihealth} uses LoRA fine-tuning for crop stress diagnosis; AgriSentinel~\cite{xu2025agrisentinel} adds differential privacy; FedReplay~\cite{li2025fedreplay} integrates frozen CLIP under FL (86.6\%, sharing only 1\% of features). RAG-based advisory generation for agriculture is unexplored in federated settings.

\textbf{Gap.} No prior work (a)~treats Federated LLM, ViT, and VLM as three separate paradigms with independent cross-paradigm comparison, or (b)~integrates a privacy-preserving RAG advisory layer jointly trained under federated constraints with per-farm FAISS stores. \subsection{Architecture Overview}

Figure~\ref{fig:system_architecture} presents the FarmFederate system architecture, comprising four integrated federated components: the LLM text encoder, the ViT image encoder, the VLM multimodal fusion and classification module, and the RAG advisory pipeline. The agricultural environment consists of heterogeneous data sources distributed across multiple farms: crop photographs from drones or smartphones, textual symptom descriptions from farmers, and IoT sensor telemetry (temperature, soil moisture, humidity, NPK levels) from field-deployed devices. Critically, all four components operate under privacy-preserving federated constraints---the FedAvg server receives only model gradients, never raw data, and the per-farm FAISS knowledge stores used by the RAG layer are never transmitted at all.

The data flow proceeds in four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Input Processing:} Text inputs are tokenized using WordPiece tokenization (max 128 tokens); images are resized to $224 \times 224$ and normalized; IoT sensor readings (temperature, soil moisture, humidity, N, P, K) are assembled into a 6-dimensional vector $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{6}$.

    \item \textbf{Encoding:} LLM encoders extract text features $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$ via [CLS] token pooling; ViT encoders produce visual features $\mathbf{h}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ through adaptive average pooling.

    \item \textbf{Fusion and Classification:} The VLM fusion module combines $\mathbf{h}_t$ and $\mathbf{h}_v$ to produce the fused representation $\mathbf{h}_f$, passed to a classification head yielding a 5-class stress prediction with class probabilities $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^{5}$.

    \item \textbf{RAG Advisory:} The RAG Query Builder projects $[\mathbf{h}_f;\,\mathbf{p};\,\mathbf{s}]$ into a 128-d query $\mathbf{q}$; the dual-encoder retriever searches the farm-local FAISS store for the top-$k$ passages; the assembled context is passed to an LLM to generate a natural-language treatment advisory. The retriever and advisory generator are trained jointly with the classifier via the combined loss $\mathcal{L}_{RAG}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dataset Description}

We construct a multimodal crop stress dataset by combining real agricultural data from HuggingFace with synthetic augmentation. The five stress classes are: \textit{water\_stress} (drought/wilting), \textit{nutrient\_def} (nutrient deficiency/chlorosis), \textit{pest\_risk} (pest infestation), \textit{disease\_risk} (fungal/bacterial disease), and \textit{heat\_stress} (thermal damage).

\subsubsection{Text Data}
Text data is sourced from \textbf{AG~News}~\cite{zhang2015character}, filtered for agricultural stress content using a vocabulary of 40 stress-specific keywords (e.g., drought, water stress, pest infestation, plant disease, heat stress, blight, nutrient deficiency). Scanning 70,219 articles produced 2,239 accepted samples across all five stress classes.

A per-class download cap of 500 is enforced during collection, yielding raw counts of 500 (water\_stress), 500 (nutrient\_def), 500 (pest\_risk), 500 (disease\_risk), and 239 (heat\_stress)---a modest 2.1:1 imbalance ratio. A rebalancing procedure oversamples minorities to the median (minimum 50 per class), yielding 2,500 fully balanced samples (500 per class).

\subsubsection{Image Data}
Image data for the stress-biased evaluation (Phase~1) is sourced from two real HuggingFace datasets:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{PlantVillage}~\cite{mohanty2016using}: 54,303 images across 38 plant disease classes, mapped to stress categories via symptom-based weights.
    \item \textbf{Beans}: 1,295 images of angular leaf spot, bean rust, and healthy beans.
\end{itemize}

For the main training pipeline (Phase~2), synthetic images with class-distinctive patterns are generated to match the rebalanced text count. Each class receives a unique color family and structural pattern (vertical stripes for water stress, concentric rings for nutrient deficiency, scattered spots for pest risk, colored lesions for disease, horizontal gradients for heat stress).

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Given a multimodal dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_t^i, \mathbf{x}_v^i, y^i)\}_{i=1}^N$ distributed across $K$ clients, the objective is to minimize the global loss while preserving data privacy:

\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{n} \mathcal{L}_k(\theta)
    \label{eq:federated_objective}
\end{equation}

subject to:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_k(\theta) &= \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_v, y) \sim \mathcal{D}_k}[\ell(\theta; \mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_v, y)] \label{eq:local_loss}\\
    \mathbf{h}_f &= f_{VLM}(\mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{h}_v) \label{eq:fusion}
\end{align}

where Eq.~(\ref{eq:local_loss}) defines the local loss at client $k$ and Eq.~(\ref{eq:fusion}) describes multimodal fusion.

\subsection{Loss Function Formulation}

The combined loss function is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{focal} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{div}
    \label{eq:combined_loss}
\end{equation}

The focal loss addresses class imbalance:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{focal} = -\alpha_c (1-p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)
    \label{eq:focal_loss}
\end{equation}

The diversity loss prevents class collapse:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{div} = \lambda \left(1 - \frac{H(\bar{p})}{H_{max}}\right)
    \label{eq:diversity_loss}
\end{equation}
where $H(\bar{p})$ is the entropy of average predictions and $H_{max} = \log(C)$ is the maximum entropy. Class weights are computed using square-root dampening with a cap at $10\times$: $\alpha_c = \min\!\big(\sqrt{n / (C \cdot n_c)},\, 10\big)$.

\subsection{Federated Learning with FedAvg}

FarmFederate uses FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}: the server broadcasts $\theta^t$ each round; each of $K{=}3$ clients runs $E{=}3$ local epochs on its private $\mathcal{D}_k$ (Dirichlet-partitioned, $\alpha{=}1.0$); the server aggregates via $\theta^{t+1} = \sum_k \frac{n_k}{n}\theta_k^{t+1}$. For the RAG retriever, aggregation uses EMA: $\theta_{ret} = \mu\,\theta_{old} + (1{-}\mu)\,\theta_{FedAvg}$ with $\mu{=}0.9$, preventing embedding drift.

\subsection{Anti-Collapse Mechanisms}

Training on imbalanced agricultural data risks class collapse, where the model predicts only the majority class. FarmFederate employs three countermeasures:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Balanced Batch Sampling:} Each training batch is constructed with equal representation from all classes via oversampling of minorities.

    \item \textbf{Diversity Loss:} A regularization term (Eq.~\ref{eq:diversity_loss}) penalizes low prediction entropy, with weight $\lambda = 1.0$ and a 70\% entropy threshold.

    \item \textbf{Early Collapse Abort:} Training is terminated after 3 consecutive epochs where $>$85\% of predictions fall into a single class, restoring the best diverse checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Complexity}

Text encoding costs $O(L^2 d)$ (self-attention, $L{=}128$, $d{=}256$); image encoding $O(P^2 d)$ ($P{=}196$ patches). Concatenation fusion is $O(d_t{+}d_v)$; Cross-Attention $O(d_t d_v)$; Q-Former $O(Q d_v)$. Communication per round is $O(K|\theta|)$; gradients-only uplink gives ${\sim}67\%$ traffic reduction vs.\ raw-data sharing. LLM encoders carry ${\sim}11$M parameters, ViTs ${\sim}1.7$M, VLMs $15.8$--$17.3$M.

\section{Federated RAG Advisory System}
\label{sec:rag}

Beyond the three federated paradigms described above, FarmFederate integrates a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) advisory layer that translates multimodal stress predictions into actionable treatment recommendations. The RAG module operates entirely on the classified output of the VLM pipeline; raw farm data is never transmitted, preserving the privacy guarantees of the federated system.

\subsection{Architecture}

Figure~\ref{fig:rag_pipeline} illustrates the federated RAG pipeline. After each farm's local model produces a stress classification, a \textbf{RAGQueryBuilder} projects the fused multimodal embedding $\mathbf{h}_f \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$, the class probability vector $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^{5}$, and the IoT sensor vector $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{6}$ into a 128-dimensional query:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{q} = \text{MLP}\bigl([\mathbf{h}_f;\,\mathbf{p};\,\mathbf{s}]\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^{128}
    \label{eq:rag_query}
\end{equation}

The query is matched against a per-farm \textbf{FarmVectorStore}---a FAISS \texttt{IndexFlatIP} index holding $k_B$ knowledge-base chunks encoded by a shared \textbf{RetrieverEncoder} (dual-encoder with DistilRoBERTa backbone). The top-$k$ retrieved passages are assembled into a context string and passed to an LLM (Google Gemini or offline fallback) to generate a natural-language advisory.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{rag_06_pipeline_diagram.png}
\caption{FarmFederate RAG advisory pipeline. The multimodal embedding, class probabilities, and IoT sensor readings are combined into a 128-d query. Per-farm FAISS vector stores retrieve the top-$k$ knowledge-base passages, which are assembled into context for LLM advisory generation. Farm-side stores are never transmitted to the server.}
\label{fig:rag_pipeline}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Joint Training}

The RAG components are trained jointly with the federated classifier using a three-term loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{RAG} = 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{cls} + 0.3\,\mathcal{L}_{ret} + 0.2\,\mathcal{L}_{gen}
    \label{eq:rag_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{cls}$ is the cross-entropy classification loss, $\mathcal{L}_{ret}$ is an InfoNCE contrastive retrieval loss that aligns query embeddings with positive document embeddings, and $\mathcal{L}_{gen}$ is a binary cross-entropy generation loss over the advisory token logits. The retriever is warm-started: federated round~1 trains classification only ($\mathcal{L}_{ret}{=}\mathcal{L}_{gen}{=}0$), then retrieval and generation losses activate from round~2 onward. After each FedAvg aggregation, the global retriever is updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA):
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{ret} = \mu\,\theta_{old} + (1-\mu)\,\theta_{FedAvg},\quad \mu=0.9
    \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The RAG evaluation uses $K{=}3$ farms, 5 federated rounds, 2 local epochs, 400 samples/farm, 10 RAG training rounds, top-$k{=}5$, query dimension 128, EMA $\mu{=}0.9$, InfoNCE retrieval loss, and BCE generation loss. Client data is split via Dirichlet ($\alpha{=}0.5$) to simulate non-IID farm distributions. After federated training, the per-farm FAISS knowledge-base indices are rebuilt using the trained retriever to ensure alignment between stored document embeddings and the learned query encoder, correcting the stale-index issue that would otherwise arise from random initialisation.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Image Quality:} While the ViT models achieve strong F1 (0.70--0.77) on synthetic images with tuned difficulty parameters, training on larger real crop imagery collections (e.g., expanded PlantVillage or drone captures) would improve generalization to field conditions and further enhance multimodal complementarity.

    \item \textbf{Text-Label Alignment:} Text data is obtained by keyword-filtering AG~News, which introduces noise in stress label assignment due to the indirect mapping from general news content to agronomic stress categories. Curated expert annotations or domain-specific agricultural corpora would improve text encoder quality.

    \item \textbf{Lightweight Architectures:} The current encoders are lightweight implementations (256-dim embeddings, 3-block CNNs) rather than full pretrained models. Using pretrained DistilBERT and ViT-Base weights with fine-tuning would improve performance.

    \item \textbf{Non-IID Sensitivity:} Federated performance degrades by ${\sim}10\%$ relative to centralized training. Adaptive aggregation strategies (FedProx, FedBN) could reduce this gap.

    \item \textbf{Scale:} Text experiments use 2,500 balanced samples (500 per class, resampled from 2,239 raw AG~News matches). Scaling to larger or more diverse corpora beyond AG~News, and to 10K+ image samples with more federated clients, would better demonstrate practical viability.

    \item \textbf{RAG Retrieval Quality:} The federated RAG advisory system achieves full knowledge-base coverage (1.00), MRR\,=\,0.800, and NDCG@5\,=\,0.894 (both meeting the ${\geq}0.70$ target). Recall@5\,=\,0.733 falls short of the ${\geq}0.85$ target. The remaining gap stems from the self-supervised InfoNCE retriever trained on synthetic template text, which provides limited hard-negative diversity. Replacing synthetic text with expert-curated agronomic advisory documents (e.g., CGIAR field reports, extension bulletins) would provide richer semantic contrast and push Recall@5 toward the target.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented FarmFederate, a framework that introduces and compares three independent federated learning paradigms for privacy-preserving crop stress detection: \textit{Federated LLM}, \textit{Federated ViT}, and \textit{Federated VLM}. Each paradigm is trained separately under FedAvg and evaluated against its own centralized baseline, enabling the first systematic cross-paradigm comparison in the agricultural federated learning literature.

Our experimental evaluation yields four key findings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Federated LLM} (MobileBERT, F1\,=\,0.608 federated / 0.604 centralized, 100.8\% retention) marginally \emph{outperforms} its centralized baseline, demonstrating that non-IID data partitioning across three clients provides implicit regularization. Despite its lower absolute accuracy, Federated LLM is the lightest deployment option, suitable for bandwidth-limited or computationally constrained agricultural settings.

    \item \textbf{Federated ViT} (ViT-Base, F1\,=\,0.816 federated / 0.825 centralized, 98.9\% retention) achieves the highest single-model F1 (0.857 centralized best) with a minimal 1.1\% federated gap, establishing vision-based federated learning as the most accurate paradigm for crop stress detection when high-quality image data is available.

    \item \textbf{Federated VLM} (Concatenation fusion, F1\,=\,0.839 federated / 0.857 centralized, 97.9\% retention) provides competitive multimodal performance. Notably, simple Concatenation outperforms all seven more complex fusion architectures (CLIP, BLIP-2, Flamingo, Cross-Attention, Gated, CoCa, Unified-IO), suggesting that feature concatenation is the most effective strategy for combining keyword-filtered text with structured image features.

    \item Anti-collapse mechanisms (diversity loss, balanced sampling, early abort) successfully prevent class collapse across all three federated paradigms and all 24 trained model variants, maintaining 100\% prediction diversity throughout federated training. The overall federated accuracy retention averages 99.2\%, far exceeding the ${\sim}90\%$ commonly reported in prior federated agricultural AI literature.
\end{enumerate}

Taken together, these results answer the central question of this paper: all three federated paradigms are remarkably federation-robust under FedAvg with $K{=}3$ non-IID clients, each retaining $>$97\% of centralized performance. Federated ViT delivers the highest absolute accuracy; Federated LLM the lightest footprint; Federated VLM the most expressive multimodal representation. The surprising finding that simple Concatenation fusion outperforms architecturally sophisticated alternatives (CLIP, BLIP-2) highlights that fusion strategy complexity should be matched to the quality and complementarity of the input modalities.

In addition to the three federated paradigms, FarmFederate incorporates a privacy-preserving Federated RAG advisory layer that retrieves domain knowledge from per-farm FAISS stores (never transmitted to the server) and generates natural-language treatment recommendations via a jointly-trained dual-encoder and LLM. The RAG pipeline achieves 100\% knowledge-base coverage, perfect classification F1, and strong retrieval quality (Recall@5\,=\,0.733, MRR\,=\,0.800, NDCG@5\,=\,0.894) under joint federated training; MRR and NDCG@5 both meet the ${\geq}0.70$ target, while Recall@5 falls short of ${\geq}0.85$ due to the self-supervised training regime and is expected to improve with real agronomic corpora.

Future work will focus on four directions: (1)~replacing keyword-filtered news text with expert-annotated field reports to unlock genuine VLM multimodal complementarity; (2)~scaling to pretrained LLM/VLM weights with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) to improve all three paradigms; (3)~deploying on edge devices via model distillation and quantization for real-time field inference; and (4)~improving the RAG retriever by training on real agricultural advisory corpora (CGIAR, agronomic extension documents) to close the gap toward the Recall@5\,$\geq$\,0.85 target.
