\begin{abstract}
This paper presents FarmFederate, a multimodal federated learning framework designed for privacy-preserving crop stress classification in precision agriculture. The proposed architecture has two main parts: the \textit{multimodal encoder} and the \textit{federated aggregator}. The multimodal encoder combines Large Language Model (LLM) encoders for text-based symptom descriptions with Vision Transformer (ViT) encoders for crop images using Vision-Language Model (VLM) fusion methods. We use the newest fusion methods, such as BLIP-2, Flamingo, and Cross-Attention, to bring together visual and textual aspects. Using Federated Averaging (FedAvg), the federated aggregator coordinates distributed learning over several agricultural sites based on the fused representations, making sure that raw data never leaves local fields. We do an extensive benchmark analysis assessing 5 LLM variants, 5 ViT variants, and 8 VLM fusion techniques across 200 model configurations. We show that FarmFederate gets a macro F1-score of 0.847 on a 5-class crop stress dataset with 5,000 multimodal samples, which is a 12.3\% improvement above unimodal baselines. The federated method gets 94.2\% of the centralized performance while cutting down on communication costs by 67\%. The suggested system surpasses cutting-edge approaches by 5.7\% in F1-score while ensuring total data privacy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{P}{recision} agriculture has emerged as a critical domain for applying advanced machine learning techniques to address global food security challenges \cite{kamilaris2018deep}. Detecting crop stress---finding things like drought, nutrient shortage, disease, and insect infestation---is important for quick action and getting the most out of your crop. Conventional methods depend on either visual examination of crop imagery or the interpretation of textual symptom reports, but seldom integrate both modes successfully.

Deep learning has led to major progress in classifying agricultural images \cite{mohanty2016using}. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and, more recently, Vision Transformers (ViTs) have both done very well on plant disease datasets. At the same time, Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown amazing skills to grasp written descriptions \cite{devlin2018bert}. In real life, though, classifying crop stress usually entails both visual signs (like leaf discoloration and wilting patterns) and written data (such environmental variables and development stage descriptions). This means that multimodal approaches are needed.

Data on farming is naturally spread out throughout many farms, regions, and institutions. Centralized data aggregation isn't possible because of privacy issues, rules on who owns data, and limited bandwidth. Federated Learning (FL) \cite{mcmahan2017communication} is a way to train models together without sharing raw data. This makes it perfect for agricultural applications where farms may not want to share their own crop data.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work seeks to address the following inquiries: (a) Is it feasible to effectively integrate LLM-based text encoders with ViT-based picture encoders for the categorization of crop stress? (b) What is the best VLM fusion approach for agricultural multimodal data? (c) Is it possible for federated learning to work as well as centralized training while still keeping privacy? The particular contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item We provide a multimodal federated learning paradigm for classifying crop stress that combines LLM text encoders, ViT image encoders, and VLM fusion mechanisms.

    \item We create a mixed-modality optimization problem that takes into account class imbalance using focal loss and diversity regularization to keep classes from collapsing.

    \item The suggested architecture has two parts: a \textit{multimodal encoder} and a \textit{federated aggregator}. These parts are meant to protect privacy while allowing distributed learning.

    \item We do a full benchmark research that looks at 200 model configurations over 5 LLM variants, 5 ViT variants, and 8 VLM fusion techniques.

    \item A lot of simulation results reveal that FarmFederate gets a macro F1-score of 0.847, which is 12.3\% better than unimodal baselines and 5.7\% better than the best approaches.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

There has been a lot of recent research in multimodal learning, federated learning, and AI for agriculture. We talk about the different ways that people use existing approaches.

\subsection{Vision-Language Models for Classification}

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have come a long way in a short amount of time thanks to transformer-based designs. CLIP \cite{radford2021learning} showed that contrastive learning on pairings of images and text can be used to convey information without any training. The Q-Former architecture for effective vision-language alignment was first introduced in BLIP-2 \cite{li2023blip}. Flamingo \cite{alayrac2022flamingo} suggested gated cross-attention as a method for incorporating visual aspects into linguistic models. CoCa \cite{yu2022coca} mixed contrastive and captioning goals. These innovations facilitate complex multimodal comprehension but have yet to be used in federated agricultural contexts.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Plant Disease Detection}

Deep learning methods for finding plant diseases have changed a lot over time. Mohanty et al. \cite{mohanty2016using} used CNNs on the PlantVillage dataset and got 99\% accuracy on 38 classes. Ferentinos \cite{ferentinos2018deep} added 58 more plant-disease pairings to this. More recently, transformer-based methods like ViT \cite{dosovitskiy2020image}, DeiT \cite{touvron2021training}, and Swin Transformer \cite{liu2021swin} have done better. Nonetheless, these studies concentrate exclusively on picture modality and centralized training.

\subsection{Federated Learning for IoT and Agriculture}

McMahan et al. \cite{mcmahan2017communication} came up with Federated Learning and the FedAvg algorithm. Later work has dealt with issues including non-IID data \cite{li2020federated}, communication efficiency \cite{konecny2016federated}, and privacy assurances \cite{bonawitz2017practical}. Durrant et al. \cite{durrant2022role} looked at cross-silo FL for agri-food in agricultural settings, while Liu et al. \cite{liu2020fedvision} came up with FedVision for finding visual objects. But no one has looked into multimodal federated learning for crop stress yet.

The data flow has three steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Input Processing:} Text inputs are broken down into tokens using model-specific tokenizers (max 128 tokens); images are changed in size, normalized, or randomly cropped or flipped.

    \item \textbf{Encoding:} LLM encoders use [CLS] token pooling to get text features $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$, and ViT encoders use visual features $\mathbf{h}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ to get visual features.

    \item \textbf{Fusion and Classification:} The VLM fusion module combines modalities to make $\mathbf{h}_f$, which is then sent to a classification head to provide a 5-class prediction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

The goal is to minimize the global loss while keeping the data private, given a multimodal dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_t^i, \mathbf{x}_v^i, y^i)\}_{i=1}^N$ that is spread out over $K$ clients. In math:

\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{n} \mathcal{L}_k(\theta)
    \label{eq:federated_objective}
\end{equation}

subject to:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_k(\theta) &= \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_v, y) \sim \mathcal{D}_k}[\ell(\theta; \mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_v, y)] \label{eq:local_loss}\\
    \mathbf{h}_f &= f_{VLM}(\mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{h}_v) \label{eq:fusion}\\
    |\theta| &\leq R_{max} \label{eq:size_constraint}\\
    \text{Latency}(\theta) &\leq D_{th} \label{eq:latency_constraint}
\end{align}

where Equation (\ref{eq:local_loss}) shows the local loss at client $k$, Equation (\ref{eq:fusion}) describes multimodal fusion, and Equations (\ref{eq:size_constraint})-(\ref{eq:latency_constraint}) provide limits on the size of the model and the time it takes to make an inference.

\subsection{Loss Function Formulation}

The combined loss function is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{focal} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{div}
    \label{eq:combined_loss}
\end{equation}

The focal loss deals with class imbalance and hard examples:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{focal} = -\alpha_c (1-p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)
    \label{eq:focal_loss}
\end{equation}

and loss of diversity stops class collapse:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{div} = \lambda \left(1 - \frac{H(\bar{p})}{H_{max}}\right)
    \label{eq:diversity_loss}
\end{equation}

where $H(\bar{p})$ is the entropy of average predictions and $H_{max} = \log(C)$ is the highest entropy.

Algorithm \ref{alg:fedavg} shows the full FedAvg process for FarmFederate.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{FarmFederate with FedAvg}
\label{alg:fedavg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Initial model $\theta^0$, clients $K$, rounds $T$, local epochs $E$
\ENSURE Trained global model $\theta^T$
\STATE Server initializes $\theta^0$ with pretrained weights
\FOR{$t = 0$ to $T-1$}
    \STATE Server broadcasts $\theta^t$ to all clients
    \FOR{each client $k \in \{1, ..., K\}$ \textbf{in parallel}}
        \STATE $\theta_k^{t+1} \leftarrow$ LocalTrain($\theta^t$, $\mathcal{D}_k$, $E$)
        \STATE // LocalTrain performs $E$ epochs of SGD
        \STATE // on local multimodal data $(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_v, y)$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $\theta^{t+1} \leftarrow \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{n} \theta_k^{t+1}$ // Weighted aggregation
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\theta^T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Multimodal Encoding Process}

Algorithm \ref{alg:encoding} shows how to encode and classify data in more than one way.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Multimodal Encoding and Classification}
\label{alg:encoding}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Text input $\mathbf{x}_t$, Image input $\mathbf{x}_v$, Model $\theta$
\ENSURE Class prediction $\hat{y}$
\STATE // Text Processing
\STATE $\text{tokens} \leftarrow \text{Tokenize}(\mathbf{x}_t)$ // Max 128 tokens
\STATE $\mathbf{h}_t \leftarrow f_{LLM}(\text{tokens})_{[\text{CLS}]}$ // Extract [CLS]
\STATE $\mathbf{h}_t \leftarrow \text{Proj}_t(\mathbf{h}_t)$ // Project to 256-d
\STATE // Image Processing
\STATE $\mathbf{x}_v \leftarrow \text{Preprocess}(\mathbf{x}_v)$ // Resize, normalize
\STATE $\mathbf{h}_v \leftarrow f_{ViT}(\mathbf{x}_v)_{[\text{CLS}]}$ // Extract [CLS]
\STATE $\mathbf{h}_v \leftarrow \text{Proj}_v(\mathbf{h}_v)$ // Project to 512-d
\STATE // Fusion
\STATE $\mathbf{h}_f \leftarrow f_{VLM}(\mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{h}_v)$ // Apply fusion
\STATE // Classification
\STATE $\text{logits} \leftarrow \text{FC}(\mathbf{h}_f)$ // 5-class output
\STATE $\hat{y} \leftarrow \arg\max(\text{softmax}(\text{logits}))$
\RETURN $\hat{y}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity Analysis}

Table \ref{tab:complexity} shows how hard it is to compute each part. The optimization problem that was created brings together parts of federated optimization and multimodal learning, which is known to be hard because of non-IID data distributions and the need for modality alignment.

\begin{table}[!t]
\centering
\caption{Computational Complexity Analysis}
\label{tab:complexity}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Component} & \textbf{Complexity} \\
\midrule

where $L$ is the length of the sequence, $P$ is the number of patches, $d$ is the hidden dimension, $Q$ is the number of queries, and $C$ is the number of classes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

FarmFederate does a great job at classifying crop stress. But there are several problems that could be fixed in the future:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Text Quality Dependency:} The text encoder needs well-formed symptom descriptions to work. Not well written or incomplete text slows down performance. One possible alternative is to use template-based input forms or LLM to help with symptom extraction.

    \item \textbf{Computational Requirements:} The best setup (213 million parameters) needs a lot of GPU memory. This makes it harder to use on edge devices. One such method is to use model distillation and quantization for edge deployment.

    \item \textbf{Non-IID Sensitivity:} Performance decreases by 5.8\% under non-IID situations relative to IID. Possible answer: the FedProx algorithm or tailored federated learning layers.

    \item \textbf{Class Overlap:} Nutrient Deficiency and Disease classes reveal 15\% of people being confused because the symptoms look the same. Possible solutions include multi-spectral imaging or temporal sequence modeling.

    \item \textbf{Language Limitation:} Right now, it only works with English text. A possible answer is to use multilingual encoders (mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa) all over the world.
\end{itemize}

Table \ref{tab:limitations} lists problems and possible remedies.

\begin{table}[!t]
\centering
\caption{Limitations and Potential Solutions}
\label{tab:limitations}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{2.5cm}p{5cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Limitation} & \textbf{Potential Solution} \\
\midrule
Text Quality Dependency & Template-based input forms; LLM-assisted symptom extraction \\
\midrule
Computational Requirements & Model distillation; quantization for edge deployment \\
\midrule
Non-IID Sensitivity & FedProx; personalized FL layers \\
\midrule
Class Overlap (Nutrient/Disease) & Multi-spectral imaging; temporal sequences \\
\midrule
English-only Text & Multilingual encoders (mBERT, XLM-R) \\

A group of farmers can use FarmFederate to share what they learn while still keeping ownership of their data. Each farm learns about local crops and their symptoms by looking at pictures of them. The federated server collects model changes, which lets knowledge move between different types of crops and areas. Some benefits are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Better stress detection from group learning
    \item Keeping a competitive edge (data privacy)
    \item Lower training expenditures for each person
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Crop Insurance Automation}

FarmFederate can be used by insurance firms to automatically analyze damage. The multimodal strategy includes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Drone images of the damaged locations
    \item Adjuster text reports that describe the situation
    \item Historical claim data that is shared across regions
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Agricultural Extension Services}

Government extension services can use FarmFederate to make mobile apps that let farmers:
\begin{itemize}

    \item Describe their symptoms in plain language
    \item Get real-time stress categorization and advice
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we introduced FarmFederate, a privacy-preserving multimodal federated learning system for the classification of crop stress. The proposed framework has two parts: a multimodal encoder and a federated aggregator. The multimodal encoder combines LLM text encoders with ViT image encoders using VLM fusion processes. This lets you fully analyze stress by looking at both visual and textual symptoms. Using FedAvg, the federated aggregator coordinates distributed learning over several agricultural sites based on the fused representations. This keeps data private while lowering the total transmission cost.

We showed that multimodal fusion works better than unimodal baselines by 12.3\% and has a macro F1-score of 0.847 with a lot of tests of 200 model combinations across 5 LLMs, 5 ViTs, and 8 VLM fusion techniques:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Multimodal fusion achieves 0.847 macro F1-score, outperforming unimodal baselines by 12.3\%.
    \item The best setup is RoBERTa + Swin + BLIP-2.
    \item Federated learning gets 94.2\% of the performance of centralized learning while keeping data private.
    \item The focus loss and diversity regularization work well together to fix class imbalance.
\end{enumerate}

FarmFederate is the only method that supports privacy-preserving federated learning and does 5.7\% better than the best approaches in F1-score.

This study illustrated the feasibility of multimodal federated learning for agricultural applications. Nonetheless, the expansion to encompass new languages and crop varieties is a prospective endeavor. So, we want to look into multilingual encoders and how to use transfer learning to new crop types. In addition, future improvements will include edge deployment with model compression and temporal sequence modeling for progressive stress detection.
