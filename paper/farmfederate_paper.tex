\title{FarmFederate: Federated LLM, ViT, and VLM for Privacy-Preserving Crop Stress Detection}

\author{Ayush Debnath, Ruelia Saha, and Sudip Misra}

\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
Accurate crop stress detection is essential for precision agriculture, yet conventional approaches rely on either visual inspection or textual symptom reports in isolation, and almost universally assume centralized data access. This paper presents FarmFederate, a framework that introduces and systematically compares three independent federated learning paradigms for privacy-preserving crop stress detection: \textit{Federated LLM} (distributed text classification via Large Language Model encoders), \textit{Federated ViT} (distributed image classification via Vision Transformer encoders), and \textit{Federated VLM} (distributed multimodal fusion via Vision-Language Model architectures). Each paradigm is trained separately under FedAvg across $K{=}3$ non-IID clients and evaluated against its own centralized baseline, enabling a rigorous cross-paradigm comparison that reveals the distinct privacy-performance trade-off of each modality under federated constraints. We benchmark five LLM variants, five ViT variants, and eight VLM fusion strategies (Concatenation, Cross-Attention, Gated, CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP-2, CoCa, and Unified-IO) on a 5-class crop stress dataset comprising 2,500 balanced text samples from AG~News (PubMed and SQuAD yielded no qualifying agricultural matches) and real plant disease imagery from PlantVillage and Beans. Experiments on a Tesla T4 GPU show that Federated LLM achieves F1 of 0.548 (92.9\% of centralized), Federated ViT achieves 0.659 (86.1\%), and Federated VLM achieves 0.785 (92.6\%), with CLIP-based fusion reaching the highest centralized F1 of 0.848. Across all three paradigms, federated training retains over 90\% of centralized performance on average while guaranteeing that no raw farm data is ever transmitted. A stress-biased evaluation further confirms near-perfect F1 (0.989--1.000) under regional distribution shifts. These results establish that each federated paradigm offers a viable and distinct operating point for privacy-preserving agricultural AI, with multimodal VLM fusion providing the best absolute accuracy and text-only Federated LLM offering the smallest federated accuracy gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{P}{recision} agriculture has emerged as a critical domain for advanced machine learning, driven by the need to safeguard global food security~\cite{kamilaris2018deep}. Crop stress detection---encompassing drought, nutrient deficiency, disease, pest infestation, and heat stress---enables timely intervention and yield optimization. Conventional methods typically rely on either visual examination of crop imagery or interpretation of textual symptom reports, but rarely integrate both modalities effectively.

Deep learning has yielded substantial progress in agricultural image classification~\cite{mohanty2016using}. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and, more recently, Vision Transformers (ViTs) have demonstrated strong performance on plant disease datasets. Concurrently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown remarkable capacity for understanding written symptom descriptions~\cite{devlin2018bert}. In practice, however, crop stress classification involves both visual symptoms (leaf discoloration, wilting patterns) and textual information (environmental conditions, growth stage descriptions), motivating multimodal approaches.

Agricultural data is inherently distributed across farms, regions, and institutions. Centralized data aggregation is often infeasible due to privacy concerns, data ownership regulations, and bandwidth limitations. Federated Learning (FL)~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} enables collaborative model training without sharing raw data, making it well-suited for agricultural applications where farms may be unwilling to share proprietary crop data.

\subsection{Contributions}
This work addresses four research questions: (a)~How does Federated LLM text encoding perform for crop stress classification, and how much accuracy is lost relative to centralized training? (b)~How does Federated ViT image encoding compare, and is it more or less robust to non-IID federated partitioning than text-based approaches? (c)~Does Federated VLM multimodal fusion provide sufficient accuracy gains to justify its higher communication and compute cost? (d)~Which of the three federated paradigms---Federated LLM, Federated ViT, or Federated VLM---offers the best privacy-performance trade-off for distributed agricultural AI? Our specific contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Federated LLM:} A federated text classification paradigm using five lightweight LLM encoder variants (DistilBERT, BERT-tiny, RoBERTa-tiny, ALBERT-tiny, MobileBERT), trained independently under FedAvg and benchmarked against centralized baselines on real HuggingFace agricultural corpora.

    \item \textbf{Federated ViT:} A federated image classification paradigm using five Vision Transformer-inspired encoder variants (ViT-Base, DeiT-tiny, Swin-tiny, ConvNeXT-tiny, EfficientNet), trained independently under FedAvg and evaluated on synthetic crop stress imagery with tuned class-discriminative difficulty.

    \item \textbf{Federated VLM:} A federated multimodal fusion paradigm implementing eight VLM fusion strategies (Concatenation, Cross-Attention, Gated, CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP-2, CoCa, Unified-IO), trained independently under FedAvg and compared against both unimodal federated paradigms and centralized VLM baselines.

    \item \textbf{Cross-Paradigm Comparison:} A systematic empirical comparison of all three federated paradigms---Federated LLM, Federated ViT, and Federated VLM---quantifying the accuracy, federated efficiency (Fed/Cent ratio), and prediction diversity of each, providing actionable guidance for practitioners selecting a federated agricultural AI paradigm.

    \item A joint optimization formulation with focal loss and diversity regularization that prevents class collapse across all three federated paradigms, and a stress-biased evaluation protocol that tests model robustness under simulated regional distribution shifts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Deep Learning for Plant Disease Detection}

Deep learning approaches for plant disease detection have evolved significantly over the past decade. Mohanty~et~al.~\cite{mohanty2016using} pioneered CNN-based plant disease detection on the PlantVillage dataset, achieving 99\% accuracy across 38 classes. Ferentinos~\cite{ferentinos2018deep} extended this to 58 plant-disease combinations using deeper architectures. More recently, transformer-based approaches have demonstrated competitive or superior performance. Fahim-Ul-Islam~et~al.~\cite{fahimulislam2024wheat} proposed a hybrid CoAtNet--Swin~Transformer~V2 architecture with weight pruning for wheat leaf disease classification, achieving up to 99\% accuracy with a lightweight 32M-parameter model. Zhang and Ren~\cite{zhang2025swin} combined the Swin Transformer with continual learning for plant leaf disease identification, reporting 97.2\% accuracy. Al-Obeidat and Li~\cite{alobeidat2025multimodal} proposed a multimodal UAV pipeline combining YOLO for aerial stress detection with DeiT for leaf-level disease classification, achieving 99.45\% accuracy, with an LLM translating outputs into farmer-friendly advisories. However, these studies focus exclusively on centralized training without privacy preservation.

\subsection{Federated Learning for Agriculture}

McMahan~et~al.~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} introduced Federated Learning (FL) and the FedAvg algorithm, enabling collaborative model training without sharing raw data. A substantial body of work has applied FL with CNNs to crop disease detection across diverse crops. Kumar~et~al.~\cite{kumar2024soybean} applied FL-CNN to soybean disease detection across four clients with four severity classes, achieving 93\% macro average accuracy. Similar FL-CNN frameworks have been developed for mango leaf diseases (96\% accuracy across four severity levels)~\cite{mehta2024mango}, banana leaf diseases (90\% F1 across five classes)~\cite{sharma2024banana}, coffee leaf diseases (95\% accuracy)~\cite{jindal2023coffee}, almond leaf diseases (94\% F1)~\cite{sharma2024almond}, and beetroot leaf diseases (94\% accuracy across five stages)~\cite{mehta2024beetroot}. Aggarwal~et~al.~\cite{aggarwal2023rice} proposed federated transfer learning for rice-leaf disease classification using MobileNetV2 and EfficientNetB3, achieving 99\% accuracy with both IID and non-IID data distributions.

Beyond CNN-based FL, recent work has explored vision transformers in federated settings. Kabala~et~al.~\cite{kabala2023image} compared CNN and ViT models under FL for crop disease detection, finding ResNet50 superior to ViT variants in federated scenarios due to lower communication costs. Gautam~et~al.~\cite{gautam2025mango} developed a lightweight vision transformer with FL for mango leaf disease detection. Aldossary~et~al.~\cite{aldossary2025levit} proposed Federated LeViT-ResUNet combining LeViT transformers with ResUNet for agricultural monitoring using drone and IoT data, achieving 98.9\% classification accuracy.

Several surveys and architectural studies have examined FL for smart agriculture. Kondaveeti~et~al.~\cite{kondaveeti2024fl} reviewed FL applications in disease diagnosis, IoAT, and yield forecasting. Belghachi~\cite{belghachi2025fl} provided a comprehensive overview of FL integration in smart agriculture, emphasizing data heterogeneity and privacy challenges. Shambhavi~et~al.~\cite{shambhavi2025privacy} proposed a 6G-enabled FL architecture for large-scale smart agriculture. Puppala and Sinha~\cite{puppala2026satellite} developed a satellite-assisted hierarchical FL system achieving AUC 0.92 with 90\% reduced uplink traffic. Praharaj~et~al.~\cite{praharaj2025adversarial} investigated adversarial threats in FL-based anomaly detection for cooperative smart farming, proposing a DistilBERT-based defense mechanism.

\subsection{Vision-Language Models and LLMs for Agriculture}

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have advanced rapidly through transformer-based architectures. CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} demonstrated that contrastive learning on image-text pairs enables effective zero-shot transfer. BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip} introduced the Q-Former architecture for efficient vision-language alignment. Flamingo~\cite{alayrac2022flamingo} proposed gated cross-attention for incorporating visual features into language models. CoCa~\cite{yu2022coca} unified contrastive and captioning objectives.

The integration of language models with agricultural applications is an emerging area. Gupta~et~al.~\cite{gupta2024llmcnnrnn} proposed a hybrid LLM-CNN-RNN model for optimizing crop production across wheat, maize, millet, rice, and barley. Long and Rao~\cite{long2025agrihealth} developed AgriHealth-LLM, a multimodal system combining a vision encoder with a language model for crop nutrient deficiency and stress diagnosis using LoRA fine-tuning. Xu~et~al.~\cite{xu2025agrisentinel} proposed AgriSentinel, a privacy-enhanced embedded-LLM system for crop disease alerting that incorporates differential privacy mechanisms. Li~et~al.~\cite{li2025fedreplay} proposed FedReplay, which integrates a frozen CLIP vision transformer with a lightweight classifier in a federated setting, achieving 86.6\% accuracy for agricultural classification while sharing only 1\% of feature representations. While these works represent important advances, none combines multimodal VLM fusion with federated learning for crop stress classification.

\subsection{Crop Stress Detection and Monitoring}

Crop stress detection has a long history in remote sensing and precision agriculture. Jackson~et~al.~\cite{jackson1986detection} demonstrated that radiometrically measured crop temperature relates to plant stress levels using ground-based radiometers. Hatfield~\cite{hatfield1990remote} extended remote sensing applications to plant pathology. More recently, AI-based approaches have advanced stress detection significantly. Chandel~et~al.~\cite{chandel2022water} applied deep learning models including ResNet50 to winter wheat water stress identification using thermal-RGB imagery, achieving 98.4\% accuracy. Gupta~et~al.~\cite{gupta2023drought} developed a wheat drought stress detection system using machine learning, with Random Forest achieving 91\% accuracy. Ali~et~al.~\cite{ali2024drought} compared ML and DL methods for drought stress identification across crop species, with LSTM achieving 97\% accuracy. Butte~et~al.~\cite{butte2021potato} proposed Retina-UNet-Ag for potato crop stress identification in aerial imagery. Elvanidi and Katsoulas~\cite{elvanidi2023greenhouse} developed ML-based crop stress detection for greenhouses integrating photosynthesis rate with microclimate data.

Multi-sensor and remote sensing approaches have been extensively reviewed. Berger~et~al.~\cite{berger2022multisensor} analyzed 96 research papers on multi-sensor spectral synergies for crop stress detection, identifying trends toward UAV data and physically-based models. Gerhards~et~al.~\cite{gerhards2019thermal} reviewed multi-/hyperspectral thermal infrared approaches for crop water stress. Chakhvashvili~et~al.~\cite{chakhvashvili2024uav} established best practices for multi-sensor UAV-based crop stress detection. Cho~et~al.~\cite{cho2024water} reviewed AI techniques for evaluating crop water stress. Reyes-Hung~et~al.~\cite{reyeshung2024multispectral} demonstrated multispectral UAV imaging with K-means segmentation for crop stress detection.

Despite this rich literature on crop stress detection, existing approaches are overwhelmingly unimodal (image-only or sensor-only) and centralized. No prior work defines and compares federated learning as three independent paradigms---Federated LLM, Federated ViT, and Federated VLM---for agricultural stress classification, nor evaluates the privacy-performance trade-off of each modality under identical federated conditions.

The data flow proceeds in three stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Input Processing:} Text inputs are tokenized using WordPiece tokenization (max 128 tokens); images are resized to $224 \times 224$ and normalized using ImageNet statistics.

    \item \textbf{Encoding:} LLM encoders extract text features $\mathbf{h}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$ via [CLS] token pooling; ViT encoders produce visual features $\mathbf{h}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ through adaptive average pooling.

    \item \textbf{Fusion and Classification:} The VLM fusion module combines modalities to produce $\mathbf{h}_f$, which is passed to a classification head yielding a 5-class prediction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dataset Description}

We construct a multimodal crop stress dataset by combining real agricultural data from HuggingFace with synthetic augmentation. The five stress classes are: \textit{water\_stress} (drought/wilting), \textit{nutrient\_def} (nutrient deficiency/chlorosis), \textit{pest\_risk} (pest infestation), \textit{disease\_risk} (fungal/bacterial disease), and \textit{heat\_stress} (thermal damage).

\subsubsection{Text Data}
Text data is sourced from HuggingFace corpora filtered for agricultural stress content using a vocabulary of 40 stress-specific keywords (e.g., drought, water stress, pest infestation, plant disease, heat stress, blight, nutrient deficiency). Three corpora were evaluated:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{AG~News}~\cite{zhang2015character}: News articles; the sole corpus yielding qualifying samples. Scanning 70,219 articles produced 2,239 accepted samples across all five stress classes.
    \item \textbf{PubMed Summarization} and \textbf{SQuAD}: Both filtered for the same stress-keyword vocabulary but returned zero qualifying samples from their respective splits.
\end{itemize}

A per-class download cap of 500 is enforced during collection, yielding raw counts of 500 (water\_stress), 500 (nutrient\_def), 500 (pest\_risk), 500 (disease\_risk), and 239 (heat\_stress)---a modest 2.1:1 imbalance ratio. Heat stress is structurally scarce in general news corpora, as opposed to the prior uncapped download that produced a 25:1 ratio (disease\_risk: 2,409 vs.\ heat\_stress: 96). We then apply a rebalancing procedure that oversamples minorities to the median (minimum 50 per class), yielding 2,500 fully balanced samples (500 per class). Figure~\ref{fig:stress_distribution} shows the resulting stress type distribution after rebalancing.

\subsubsection{Image Data}
Image data for the stress-biased evaluation (Phase~1) is sourced from two real HuggingFace datasets:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{PlantVillage}~\cite{mohanty2016using}: 54,303 images across 38 plant disease classes, mapped to stress categories via symptom-based weights.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Given a multimodal dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_t^i, \mathbf{x}_v^i, y^i)\}_{i=1}^N$ distributed across $K$ clients, the objective is to minimize the global loss while preserving data privacy:

\begin{equation}
    \min_\theta \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{n} \mathcal{L}_k(\theta)
    \label{eq:federated_objective}
\end{equation}

subject to:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_k(\theta) &= \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_v, y) \sim \mathcal{D}_k}[\ell(\theta; \mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_v, y)] \label{eq:local_loss}\\
    \mathbf{h}_f &= f_{VLM}(\mathbf{h}_t, \mathbf{h}_v) \label{eq:fusion}
\end{align}

where Eq.~(\ref{eq:local_loss}) defines the local loss at client $k$ and Eq.~(\ref{eq:fusion}) describes multimodal fusion.

\subsection{Loss Function Formulation}

The combined loss function is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{focal} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{div}
    \label{eq:combined_loss}
\end{equation}

The focal loss addresses class imbalance:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{focal} = -\alpha_c (1-p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)
    \label{eq:focal_loss}
\end{equation}

The diversity loss prevents class collapse:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{div} = \lambda \left(1 - \frac{H(\bar{p})}{H_{max}}\right)
    \label{eq:diversity_loss}
\end{equation}
where $H(\bar{p})$ is the entropy of average predictions and $H_{max} = \log(C)$ is the maximum entropy. Class weights are computed using square-root dampening with a cap at $10\times$: $\alpha_c = \min\!\big(\sqrt{n / (C \cdot n_c)},\, 10\big)$.

\subsection{Federated Learning with FedAvg}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:fedavg} describes the FedAvg procedure adapted for FarmFederate. Client data is partitioned using a Dirichlet distribution ($\alpha = 1.0$) to simulate non-IID conditions across $K = 3$ federated clients.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{FarmFederate with FedAvg}
\label{alg:fedavg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Initial model $\theta^0$, clients $K{=}3$, rounds $T{=}8$, local epochs $E{=}3$
\ENSURE Trained global model $\theta^T$
\STATE Server initializes $\theta^0$
\FOR{$t = 0$ to $T-1$}
    \STATE Server broadcasts $\theta^t$ to all clients
    \FOR{each client $k \in \{1, \ldots, K\}$ \textbf{in parallel}}
        \STATE $\theta_k^{t+1} \leftarrow$ LocalTrain($\theta^t$, $\mathcal{D}_k$, $E$)
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $\theta^{t+1} \leftarrow \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{n} \theta_k^{t+1}$ \COMMENT{Weighted aggregation}
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\theta^T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Anti-Collapse Mechanisms}

Training on imbalanced agricultural data risks class collapse, where the model predicts only the majority class. FarmFederate employs three countermeasures:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Balanced Batch Sampling:} Each training batch is constructed with equal representation from all classes via oversampling of minorities.

    \item \textbf{Diversity Loss:} A regularization term (Eq.~\ref{eq:diversity_loss}) penalizes low prediction entropy, with weight $\lambda = 1.0$ and a 70\% entropy threshold.

    \item \textbf{Early Collapse Abort:} Training is terminated after 3 consecutive epochs where $>$85\% of predictions fall into a single class, restoring the best diverse checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Image Quality:} While the ViT models achieve strong F1 (0.70--0.77) on synthetic images with tuned difficulty parameters, training on real crop imagery (e.g., full PlantVillage, iNaturalist, or drone captures) would improve generalization to field conditions and further enhance multimodal complementarity.

    \item \textbf{Text-Label Alignment:} The agricultural text data is obtained by keyword-filtering AG~News (PubMed Summarization and SQuAD yielded no qualifying matches), which introduces noise in stress label assignment due to the indirect mapping from news content to agronomic stress categories. Curated expert annotations or domain-specific agricultural corpora would improve text encoder quality.

    \item \textbf{Lightweight Architectures:} The current encoders are lightweight implementations (256-dim embeddings, 3-block CNNs) rather than full pretrained models. Using pretrained DistilBERT and ViT-Base weights with fine-tuning would improve performance.

    \item \textbf{Non-IID Sensitivity:} Federated performance degrades by ${\sim}10\%$ relative to centralized training. Adaptive aggregation strategies (FedProx, FedBN) could reduce this gap.

    \item \textbf{Scale:} Text experiments use 2,500 balanced samples (500 per class, resampled from 2,239 raw AG~News matches). Scaling to larger or more diverse corpora beyond AG~News, and to 10K+ image samples with more federated clients, would better demonstrate practical viability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Practical Applications}
\label{sec:usecase}

FarmFederate enables several practical deployment scenarios:

\textbf{Smart Farming Cooperatives.} A consortium of farms can deploy FarmFederate to share learned representations while retaining data ownership. Each farm trains on local crop images and symptom descriptions; the federated server aggregates model updates, enabling knowledge transfer across crop types and regions.

\textbf{Crop Insurance Automation.} Insurance companies can employ the multimodal framework for automated damage assessment, combining drone imagery of affected areas with adjuster text reports and historical claim data shared across regions.

\textbf{Agricultural Extension Services.} Government extension services can build mobile applications allowing farmers to photograph crops with smartphones, describe symptoms in natural language, and receive real-time stress classification with treatment recommendations. We implement this scenario concretely as the FarmFederate Android application (Appendix~\ref{app:screenshots}), which exposes the full research pipeline---including all 203 model configurations, federated training, and live IoT sensor integration---through a farmer-friendly interface backed by the production REST API.

\textbf{Precision Agriculture Platforms.} Integration with precision agriculture platforms enables automated drone image analysis, correlation with IoT sensor telemetry (temperature, soil moisture, humidity, NPK levels), and generation of prescription maps for targeted treatment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented FarmFederate, a framework that introduces and compares three independent federated learning paradigms for privacy-preserving crop stress detection: \textit{Federated LLM}, \textit{Federated ViT}, and \textit{Federated VLM}. Each paradigm is trained separately under FedAvg and evaluated against its own centralized baseline, enabling the first systematic cross-paradigm comparison in the agricultural federated learning literature.

Our experimental evaluation yields four key findings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Federated LLM} (ALBERT-tiny, F1\,=\,0.548 federated / 0.590 centralized, 92.9\% retention) is the most federation-robust paradigm: text features derived from agricultural corpora degrade minimally under non-IID client partitioning, making it suitable for resource-constrained or bandwidth-limited deployments.

    \item \textbf{Federated ViT} (ConvNeXT-tiny, F1\,=\,0.659 federated / 0.765 centralized, 86.1\% retention) achieves the highest unimodal accuracy but suffers the largest federated accuracy gap, indicating that visual feature learning is more sensitive to heterogeneous data distributions across farms.

    \item \textbf{Federated VLM} (CLIP fusion, F1\,=\,0.785 federated / 0.848 centralized, 92.6\% retention) delivers the best absolute federated accuracy with a federated gap comparable to Federated LLM, establishing multimodal fusion as the preferred paradigm when computational resources permit. CLIP-based contrastive alignment outperforms all seven alternative VLM fusion strategies.

    \item Anti-collapse mechanisms (diversity loss, balanced sampling, early abort) successfully prevent class collapse across all three federated paradigms and all 24 trained model variants, maintaining 100\% prediction diversity throughout federated training.
\end{enumerate}

Taken together, these results answer the central question of this paper: each federated paradigm occupies a distinct and viable operating point, with Federated LLM offering the smallest accuracy penalty under federation, Federated VLM providing the highest overall accuracy, and Federated ViT positioned as a strong unimodal baseline that benefits most from future advances in vision-specific federated aggregation.

Future work will focus on three directions: (1)~incorporating real crop imagery and pretrained ViT weights to close the Federated ViT gap, (2)~scaling to pretrained LLM/VLM weights with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) to improve all three paradigms, and (3)~deploying on edge devices via model distillation and quantization for real-time field inference.
