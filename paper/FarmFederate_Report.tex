\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{FarmFederate} is the \emph{first} framework to define, independently train, and
directly compare three federated learning paradigms for multi-class crop stress detection,
all trained under identical FedAvg conditions ($K{=}3$ clients, $T{=}8$ rounds, $E{=}3$ local
epochs) with no raw farm data ever transmitted.

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{The Problem}

Crop stress---encompassing drought, nutrient deficiency, pest infestation, disease, and heat
damage---causes 20--40\% yield loss globally.
Existing AI solutions have two critical shortcomings:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Centralized:} farms must upload raw images or text to a shared server,
        violating data privacy and ownership rights.
  \item \textbf{Unimodal:} methods use images \emph{or} text in isolation, missing
        complementary diagnostic signals.
  \item \textbf{No paradigm comparison:} no prior work trains Federated LLM, ViT, and VLM
        separately and evaluates which offers the best privacy-utility trade-off.
\end{itemize}

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Three Independent Federated Paradigms}

Each paradigm is trained and federated \textbf{independently}---the results are then compared
directly to reveal their distinct operating points.

% ── Paradigm 1: Federated LLM ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{paradigmbox}[linecolor=llmblue]
\noindent{\large\bfseries\textcolor{llmblue}{Paradigm 1 --- Federated LLM}}
\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{Input:} Farmer-written symptom text
(e.g., \textit{``leaves yellowing, dry cracked soil''}).

\noindent\textbf{Models:} DistilBERT, BERT-tiny, RoBERTa-tiny, ALBERT-tiny, MobileBERT.

\smallskip
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Centralized F1} & \textbf{Federated F1} \\
\midrule
DistilBERT    & 0.571 & 0.530 \\
BERT-tiny     & 0.585 & 0.543 \\
RoBERTa-tiny  & 0.585 & 0.541 \\
\rowcolor{llmlight}
ALBERT-tiny \textbf{(best)} & \textbf{0.590} & \textbf{0.548} \\
MobileBERT    & 0.535 & 0.497 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Key finding:} Smallest federated accuracy gap (7.1\%)---text features
are \emph{robust} to non-IID client partitioning.
Ideal for bandwidth-limited or low-compute deployments.

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\enspace
Input text: \textit{``brown spots on leaf margins, leaves curling upward, soil dry''} \\
Federated LLM prediction $\to$ \textbf{Disease Risk} \hfill (61\% confidence)
\end{examplebox}
\end{paradigmbox}

\section{Dataset}

The 5-class crop stress dataset is built from real HuggingFace corpora and synthetic imagery:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{lXl}
\toprule
\textbf{Class} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Visual Signature} \\
\midrule
Water Stress    & Drought, wilting, stomatal closure      & Vertical stripes, pale green \\
Nutrient Def.   & Chlorosis, stunted growth               & Concentric rings, yellowing \\
Pest Risk       & Holes, sticky residue, webbing          & Scattered dark spots \\
Disease Risk    & Fungal/bacterial lesions                & Coloured lesions, blight \\
Heat Stress     & Leaf scorch, tip burn                   & Horizontal gradients, brown tips \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Text:} AG~News $+$ PubMed $+$ SQuAD filtered for agriculture
$\to$ $\sim$1,089 balanced samples after rebalancing (original 25:1 imbalance corrected
by capping majority at $2\times$ median and oversampling minorities to median).

\noindent\textbf{Images:} PlantVillage $+$ Beans (real) $+$ synthetic class-distinctive
patterns (pattern intensity 25--50\%, 90\% pattern presence, 20\% cross-class confusion).

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Anti-Collapse Mechanisms}

Severe class imbalance (25:1) causes models to collapse to predicting only 1--2 classes.
FarmFederate prevents this across \emph{all three paradigms} via:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Balanced Batch Sampling} --- equal class representation per batch via
        minority oversampling using the maximum class count for batch size.
  \item \textbf{Diversity Loss} ---
        $\mathcal{L}_{div} = \lambda\!\left(1 - H(\bar{p})/H_{\max}\right)$
        with $\lambda{=}1.0$ and 70\% entropy threshold; adds confidence penalty.
  \item \textbf{Early Collapse Abort} --- terminates training after 3 consecutive
        epochs where $>$85\% of predictions fall into one class; restores best checkpoint.
  \item \textbf{Focal Loss} --- $\mathcal{L}_{focal} = -\alpha_c(1-p_t)^\gamma\log p_t$
        with $\gamma{=}2.0$ and class weights capped at $10\times$
        (sqrt dampening: $\alpha_c = \min(\sqrt{n/Cn_c},\,10)$).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Result:} \textbf{100\% prediction diversity} maintained across all 24
trained models and all three federated paradigms.

\section{Novelty}

No prior work has:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Defined \fedllm{}, \fedvit{}, and \fedvlm{} as \emph{three distinct federated paradigms}.
  \item Trained each paradigm \textbf{independently} under identical FedAvg conditions.
  \item Provided a \textbf{cross-paradigm comparison} quantifying the per-paradigm
        privacy-utility trade-off.
  \item Applied any of these to \textbf{multi-class crop stress} (5 classes, not binary disease).
\end{enumerate}

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Practical Use Case}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{End-to-end scenario:} A farmer in Punjab photographs yellowing maize leaves and
types \textit{``leaves pale yellow, lower leaves affected first, slow growth''} into the
FarmFederate Android app.

\medskip
The app encodes the image (ViT encoder) and text (LLM encoder) \emph{locally}, fuses them
via CLIP contrastive alignment, and sends only the \textbf{model update}---not the raw
photo or text---to the federated server.
After aggregating updates from three farms (Punjab, Kerala, UP), the global model predicts:

\medskip
\centerline{\Large\textcolor{vitgreen}{\bfseries Nutrient Deficiency} \quad (78\% confidence)}

\medskip
The app recommends: \textit{``Apply 30\,kg urea/acre. Re-evaluate in 10 days.''}

\medskip
\textbf{Privacy guarantee:} The crop photograph never left the farmer's device.
\end{examplebox}

\section{Conclusion}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{\textcolor{llmblue}{Federated LLM}} --- most federation-robust (92.9\% retention),
        lowest compute cost; best for text-rich, bandwidth-constrained deployments.
  \item \textbf{\textcolor{vitgreen}{Federated ViT}} --- highest unimodal centralized accuracy
        (F1\,=\,0.765) but most sensitive to non-IID heterogeneity; future work should apply
        FedBN or pretrained weights with LoRA.
  \item \textbf{\textcolor{vlmred}{Federated VLM}} --- best overall federated performance
        (F1\,=\,0.785, 92.6\% retention); CLIP fusion recommended when compute and
        dual-modality data are available.
\end{itemize}

\noindent FarmFederate demonstrates that the choice of federated paradigm is not merely
a modality preference but a \textbf{fundamental design decision} with distinct
privacy-utility implications, providing practitioners with clear, empirically grounded
guidance for deploying federated agricultural AI.
