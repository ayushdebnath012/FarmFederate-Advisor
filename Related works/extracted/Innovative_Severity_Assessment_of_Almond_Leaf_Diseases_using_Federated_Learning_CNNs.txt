Abstract— The study details implementing a federated learning 
framework using a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to 
assess the severity of almond leaf diseases across a group of six 
different customers, placing it at the forefront of agritech 
innovation. This research methodically stratifies the condition 
into four severity categories - Mild (1-25%), Moderate (26-
50%), Severe (51-75%), and Very Severe (76-100%) - giving a 
granular lens through which the affliction's evolution is 
examined. The method is new because it makes use of federated 
learning. 
This 
model 
safeguards 
the 
privacy 
and 
decentralization of data while drawing on the wisdom of many 
via the use of disparate datasets. Central to the approach is 
federated averaging to combine the results of locally trained 
CNN models on individual client datasets. Using the specifics of 
each client's data, this method generates a unified model that 
incorporates a thorough knowledge of illness trends. The 
outcome analysis proves the approach's efficacy and provides 
new and exciting perspectives. In terms of precision (93.93%), 
recall (93.98%), and F1-score (93.94%), Client qg_1 stood out 
as the gold standard. There was a wide range of results from one 
client to the next, with qg_2 maintaining a solid posture 
(Precision: 88.51%, Recall: 88.54%, F1-score: 88.50%, 
Accuracy: 94%) and qg_3 struggling (Precision: 65.44%, 
Recall: 65.49%, F1-score: 65.43%, Accuracy: 83%). Metrics 
such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-scores for clients 
qg_4, qg_5, and qg_6 all ranged in the upper echelons, which is 
to say, very well. This journey of discovery not only sheds light 
on the efficacy of federated learning in agricultural disease 
detection but paves the way for more studies in distant and 
decentralized data settings. 
Keywords: Almond leaves, CNN, distributed learning, 
Disease bifurcations, severity analysis.

technologies are federated learning and Convolutional Neural 
Networks (CNN) [1]. 
The cutting-edge technique of federated learning, which 
allows machine learning models to be trained across several 
devices or servers without centralizing data, is at the heart of 
the study. This method has great potential for efficient illness

enhanced data privacy. Federated learning is critical in 
democratizing data-driven insights since it rejects the need to 
centralize data and enables model training across devices [2].

In the input layer, photos of almond leaves with 365 × 365-
pixel dimensions and three RGB color channels enter the 
network. Convolutional Layers: These layers identify 
characteristics in the input picture. The size of the kernel or 
filter is 3x3. The number of filters (depth of the feature map) 
rises as we go further into the network (from Conv1 to 
Conv3), allowing the network to recognize more complicated 
features. Layers using Max Pooling: Following each 
convolutional layer, a 2x2 pooling size is used to apply max 
pooling, which lowers the spatial dimensions of the feature 
map by half and aids in achieving translational invariance. 
Flatten: The feature map is converted into a one-dimensional 
vector before being sent to the fully linked layers. Fully 
Connected Layers: The output traverses two dense layers after 
it has been flattened. 512 neurons in the first layer have a 
ReLU activation function. With 13 neurons or 13 classes of 
illnesses, the second layer is the output layer. It utilizes a 
Softmax activation function to produce probabilities for each 
category. A batch of datasets, including 2595 photos, will be 
fed into the network. After passing through the previously 
described layers, the model will forecast the class probabilities 
for each image in the batch. Batch processing facilitates data 
processing in parallel, speeding up and improving training 
effectiveness. Almond leaf photos should be reliably 
classified into one of the 13 disease classes thanks to the 
architecture's ability to extract pertinent elements, as shown in
