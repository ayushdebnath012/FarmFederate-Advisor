Crop diseases pose significant threats to global food security, agricultural productivity, and sustainable farming
practices, directly affecting farmers’ livelihoods and economic stability. To address the growing need for
effective crop disease management, AI-based disease alerting systems have emerged as promising tools by
providing early detection and actionable insights for timely intervention. However, existing systems often
overlook critical aspects such as data privacy, market pricing power, and farmer-friendly usability, leaving
farmers vulnerable to privacy breaches and economic exploitation. To bridge these gaps, we propose AgriSen-
tinel, the first Privacy-Enhanced Embedded-LLM Crop Disease Alerting System. AgriSentinel incorporates a
differential privacy mechanism to protect sensitive crop image data while maintaining classification accuracy.
Its lightweight deep learning-based crop disease classification model is optimized for mobile devices, ensuring
accessibility and usability for farmers. Additionally, the system includes a fine-tuned, on-device large language
model (LLM) that leverages a curated knowledge pool to provide farmers with specific, actionable sugges-
tions for managing crop diseases, going beyond simple alerting. Comprehensive experiments validate the
effectiveness of AgriSentinel, demonstrating its ability to safeguard data privacy, maintain high classification
performance, and deliver practical, actionable disease management strategies. AgriSentinel offers a robust,
farmer-friendly solution for automating crop disease alerting and management, ultimately contributing to
improved agricultural decision-making and enhanced crop productivity.

RELATED WORK
This section reviews the related works of crop disease alerting systems, agricultural large language
models, and privacy-enhanced AI approaches.
2.1
Crop Disease Alerting Approaches
Crop disease alerting approaches can be broadly categorized into context-aware models and data-
driven models. (1) Context-aware models [22, 26] integrate broader contextual information, such
as regional agricultural practices and environmental factors. For instance, real-time sensor inputs
and historical weather data play a crucial role in improving the accuracy of disease forecasting.
Additionally, social media data can provide insights into local agricultural sentiment, offering a
holistic understanding of crop health dynamics. (2) Data-driven models [30, 42] leverage diverse
data sources, applying machine learning and deep learning techniques for disease prediction. CNNs
have been widely used for classifying crop disease images [12]. Techniques like feature-level fusion
and ensemble learning enhance predictive accuracy by combining outputs from multiple data-
driven models [20]. Furthermore, attention mechanisms improve the interpretability of extracted
features [32], while representation learning aids in identifying critical patterns for precise crop
disease prediction.
2.2
Agricultural Large Language Models
Modern LLMs like GPT and BERT have revolutionized data processing and generation with their
exceptional understanding and adaptability [36]. Recently, these models have begun transforming
the agricultural sector by addressing complex challenges such as disease detection, crop mon-
itoring, and strategic decision-making [43]. In agriculture, LLMs excel at extracting structured
information from unstructured data sources, including reports, news articles, and farmer records,
providing actionable insights that enhance various agricultural tasks [4]. Furthermore, these mod-
els facilitate automated decision-making by integrating with other AI systems, streamlining the
identification and management of crops and pests. Their few-shot learning capabilities are par-
ticularly advantageous in agriculture, where data collection is often time-intensive and costly.
However, implementing LLMs in agriculture poses significant challenges. These include the need
for high-quality, diverse datasets and the computational resources required to train and deploy the
models [2]. Additionally, ethical concerns, such as data privacy and security, add further complexity
to their practical adoption [38].
2.3
Privacy-Enhanced AI Approaches
Preserving data privacy is critical in the era of big data, enabling meaningful analysis and model
training while safeguarding sensitive information. Four key methodologies are commonly em-
ployed for privacy protection in AI. (1) Differential privacy, widely adopted in machine learning
frameworks, introduces random noise to data or model outputs to prevent the identification of
individual data points [1]. (2) Federated learning enhances privacy by training models across
decentralized devices or servers holding local data, eliminating the need for data sharing [34]. (3)
Homomorphic encryption enables computations on encrypted data, ensuring sensitive information
remains concealed [14]. (4) Secure multi-party computation (SMPC) facilitates privacy-preserving
collaborative learning by allowing multiple parties to compute functions over private inputs [16].

As the demand for privacy in data-driven applications grows, the integration of these techniques

The development of a privacy-enhanced LLM-based crop disease alerting system presents two
significant technical challenges. First, designing a lightweight and user-friendly system that allows
farmers to easily adopt and leverage the transformative capabilities of large language models for
effective crop disease management is inherently complex. Second, ensuring privacy-enhanced
training for agricultural LLMs is critical, as inadequately protected crop data could be exploited
to infer production levels, potentially undermining farmers’ market pricing power and causing
economic losses.

AGRISENTINEL MODEL
We propose an AgriSentinel framework, illustrated in Fig. 1, as a privacy-enhanced on-device
system for comprehensive crop disease alerting that seamlessly integrates differential privacy,
deep learning, and LLM. The framework begins with a Gaussian noise-based differential privacy
mechanism to safeguard sensitive crop data, introducing controlled noise to achieve a balance
between privacy protection and the preservation of critical diagnostic features. The obfuscated data
is subsequently processed by a lightweight deep learning model designed for efficient and accurate
disease classification, leveraging advanced architectural components such as convolutional layers
and ReLU activation to optimize computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. Building upon
these predictive results, the framework incorporates a fine-tuned on-device LLM, enriched with
a curated knowledge pool, to generate precise and actionable disease management strategies. To
sum up, AgriSentinel offers farmers a lightweight and user-friendly tool to enhance crop health
management while addressing key challenges in usability and data protection.

Differential Privacy Noise Injection Mechanism
We propose a differential privacy mechanism to protect crop data by employing Gaussian noise
as an effective obfuscation technique. Gaussian noise introduces controlled random variations to
the pixel values of an image, masking identifiable features while preserving the image’s utility
for analysis. This approach strikes a balance between safeguarding data privacy and retaining the
essential diagnostic information required for accurate disease pattern recognition. The process of

3.2
Deep Learning-based Crop Disease Classification
After acquiring the obfuscated crop images dataset, we have developed a lightweight deep learning
model tailored for crop disease classification. This model is structured as follows: (1) Data Normal-
ization: An essential preprocessing step, data normalization adjusts each pixel value by dividing by
255. This scales the image pixel values to a range of [0, 1], ensuring a uniform distribution of input
values. Such normalization not only stabilizes the learning process but also accelerates convergence
and improves the accuracy of the model by minimizing the impact of variations in image bright-
ness and contrast. (2) Convolutional and Pooling Layers: These layers are crucial for extracting
spatial and local features from the images of rice diseases. The convolutional layers detect these
features, while the pooling layers reduce the dimensionality of the data, which helps in handling
overfitting. (3) Dense and Activation Layers: Positioned after the convolutional and pooling layers,
the dense layer, coupled with a ReLU activation layer in Eq. (2), integrates the extracted features.
This combination is pivotal for the complex decision-making required to accurately predict disease
categories. By streamlining the architecture with these elements, our model remains lightweight
yet powerful enough to effectively classify various crop diseases based on visual cues.
