Abstract
Precision agriculture requires scalable, accurate monitoring solutions to bridge the gap from macro-level field awareness down to
leaf-level diagnostics. This paper presents a novel multimodal pipeline that integrates Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) imagery
with advanced deep-learning models, achieving broad-spectrum stress detection and fine-grained disease classification. A CNN
initially established a foundational accuracy but struggled to capture the subtle signatures of different diseases. Switching to Vision
Transformers (ViTs) improved performance, yet computational overhead and data requirements posed challenges. Later, we moved
to DeiT with extensive hyperparameter tuning and data augmentations that gave an astonishing 99.45% accuracy on the multi-class
plant disease dataset. To complement the close-up acumen of DeiT, we used YOLO from an aerial view to rapidly identify stressed
versus healthy crop regions from real-time UAV footage. This two-tiered approach, wherein YOLO is used for aerial scanning and
DeiT for leaf-level diagnoses, offers an unprecedented level of precision with scalability. Finally, complex output is translated by
an LLM into farmer-friendly advisories to ensure immediate actionable insights. Our integrated framework sets a new benchmark
in UAV-driven precision agriculture by balancing model sophistication, computational feasibility, and end-user interpretability.

1. Introduction
Agriculture is the backbone of food security at a global scale; however, it is increasingly facing challenges in terms
of increased demand, climate change, and resource constraints. Traditional methods of crop monitoring, such as man-
ual inspections, are not only labor-intensive and time-consuming but also lack the precision and scalability required
to address these challenges effectively. The advent of unmanned aerial vehicles together with efficient, advanced ma-
chine learning models enables a new frontier in precision agriculture through efficient, accurate, and large-scale crop
monitoring.
This paper introduces a multimodal pipeline that leverages UAV imagery with state-of-the-art deep-learning models
in tackling the dual tasks of broad-spectrum stress detection and fine-grained disease classification. It uses YOLO V8
for detecting aerial stresses and Data-Efficient Image Transformers (DeiT) for high-fidelity disease classification at
the leaf level, which results in a holistic system that is balanced for scalability, accuracy, and computational efficiency.
More importantly, LLM translation of technical output into actionable insight for the farmer bridges high technology
with practical applicability.
This paper is divided into five major sections, giving an all-inclusive overview of our work. The section of Related
Work reviews current approaches to precision agriculture, including both the application of UAV imagery and deep
learning models. We discuss the strengths and limitations of prior works, establishing the need for a unified pipeline
that integrates the aerial and close-up perspectives. The Methodology section elaborates in detail on the datasets, pre-
processing techniques, and model architectures in our system. We have elaborated on the training processes involved
with YOLO V8 and DeiT, with particular emphasis on hyperparameter tuning and data augmentation techniques for
which their performances were significantly improved.
In the Results section, the performance metrics used to quantify the developed pipeline of the proposed model
include accuracy, mean average precision, and computational efficiency, among others. This will depict the overall
performance regarding aerial stress detection and leaf-level disease classification. A general discussion is presented,
showing such results’ analysis by underlining the strengths of this approach in a multimodal pipeline, considering
such practical limitations as partially overlapping regions within crops and processing. Finally, the section entitled
”Conclusion and Future Work” summarizes our contributions and gives directions for future work: deployment on
edge devices, multi-temporal analysis of dynamic crop monitoring, and further optimization for scalability.
The aim is to set a new benchmark in the domain of UAV-driven precision agriculture and, hence, propose a
robust, scalable, and user-friendly pipeline concerning pressing modern farming needs. We have presented the power
of various advanced machine learning models integrated with UAV imagery to present its strength in transforming the
agriculture landscape using this structured approach.
2. Related Work
2.1. UAV-Based Crop Monitoring
UAV imagery has dramatically improved farm surveillance, enabling real-time, large-scale assessments. Early
approaches often relied on vegetation indices, such as the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), which
provide useful insights into crop health but lack the granularity needed for detailed disease diagnostics [1]. Recent
advances in deep learning and computer vision have enhanced UAV-based crop monitoring capabilities by allowing
pixel-level segmentation and object detection in agricultural contexts [2].
2.2. Deep Learning in Plant Pathology
Kamilaris and Prenafeta-Bold´u [3] discussed that although CNNs perform excellently for plant disease classifica-
tion, their capabilities are restricted to large data sets and UAV imagery due to scalability and high computational
load. Our hybrid approach DeiT uses self-attention mechanisms, which efficiently model global or field-level patterns
with much reduced typical heavy computation associated with Transformers; thus, making the feasibility of DeiT for
UAV datasets possible.

2.3. Data-Efficient Transformers (DeiT)
Dosovitsky et al. [4] proposed the Vision Transformers, which, while performing remarkably well on large-scale
data, have a very high computational cost and are impractical for real-world use. DeiT [5] by Touvron et al. is a
data-efficient variant of ViTs that achieves performance parity while having much lower computational costs. While
Dosovitsky et al. had originally presented their work in the context of general-purpose vision, our adaptation Naser
Al-Obeidat, Zimo Li / Procedia Computer Science 00 (2019) 000–000 3 is fine-tuned for precision agriculture in view
of its effectiveness in processing images from UAVs to identify stress and diseases.
2.4. Aerial Object Detection with YOLO
Reis et al. [6] demonstrated the utility of YOLO V8 for real-time UAV-based object detection, achieving 95%
accuracy, but focused primarily on object localization, such as identifying plant rows or sections, rather than disease
classification. Our approach extends Reis et al.’s work by combining YOLO V8 with DeiT and CNNs. YOLO is
used for real-time object detection, focusing on plant-level health status, while DeiT and CNNs specialize in disease
classification. Diverse complementary datasets, such as Plant Disease Identification and Plant Health Tracker, are used
to further train these models for both close-up and field-level imagery.
2.5. Interpretability via LLMs
This is also where Large Language Models (LLMs) in GPT-based architectures [7], have helped generate end-
friendly advisories from complex technical outputs. Integrating LLMs with computer vision pipelines allows the
derivation of actionable insights for non-expert users, closing the gap from advanced analytics to practical applica-
tions.
Existing research highlights the transformative potential of UAV imagery and deep learning in precision agricul-
ture. Kamilaris and Prenafeta-Boldu (2018) demonstrated the effectiveness of CNNs for plant disease classification
but noted challenges with large datasets and UAV scalability. Reis et al. (2023) showcased YOLO V8’s real-time
object detection capabilities for UAV imagery, achieving high accuracy in identifying plant rows but lacking disease-
specific analysis. While Vision Transformers (ViTs) introduced by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020) offered global attention
mechanisms for improved image classification, their high computational requirements make them impractical for field
applications. DeiT, a data-efficient ViT variant, addresses these challenges but has yet to be applied comprehensively
in agriculture. My work combines these advancements into a unified pipeline that leverages YOLO V8 for macro-level
stress detection and DeiT for fine-grained disease classification. By integrating CNNs and data augmentation strate-
gies, the pipeline bridges scalability and computational efficiency gaps, while incorporating Large Language Models
(LLMs) ensures actionable insights for end-users, setting a new benchmark in UAV-driven precision agriculture.

3. Methodology
Our proposed pipeline operates in two distinct levels: phases—macro-level stress detection and micro-level disease
classification—followed by an interpretability layer.
3.1. UAV Imagery and YOLO-Based Stress Detection
A UAV (DJI Mavic Mini 2 SE) collects high-resolution images of cornfields. YOLO (we tested the YOLOv8 and
YOLOv11 variants) processes these images, classifying the field areas as ’stressed’ or ’healthy’. By incorporating
enhancements such as Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and Path Aggregation Network (PAN), YOLO provides great
detection across different scales and light conditions. The result is a set of geolocated hotspots requiring further
scrutiny, dramatically reducing the field area needing detailed examination.
3.2. Close-Up Disease Analysis: From CNN to DeiT
For each stressed hot spot, close-up images of leaves are captured and passed through the disease classification
models. Initially, we deployed a ResNet-18 CNN that achieved 86% accuracy. Introducing a baseline ViT improved
accuracy to 88%, confirming that global attention captures subtle disease cues about diseases that often get missed
by CNNs.

Yet, the computational demands of ViT were very high. To balance efficiency and accuracy, we employed DeiT.
We conducted extensive hyperparameter tuning: learning rates from 0.0001 to 0.00005, batch sizes from 16 to 64, and
weight decay parameters were tuned for better performance. A key innovation was the use of advanced data augmen-
tations: ±30◦rotations, brightness and contrast shifts, and scaling transformations. These augmentations introduce
realistic variability and enable DeiT to learn robust representations which generalize across diverse field conditions.
Careful optimization resulted in DeiT attaining an extremely high accuracy of 99.45% on the multi-class Kag-
gle plant disease dataset with more than 87,000 images. The intrinsically data-efficient architecture of DeiT, along
with these refinements, kept computational overhead low enough for on-farm servers or even mobile processing
units—crucial for immediate interventions and rapid feedback loops.
3.3. UAV Autonomous Flight and Video Transmission Bridge
Manual control of UAVs for field coverage is both challenging and inefficient. To address this, we integrated
various ROS packages with the DJI SDK to enable autonomous flight. This system ensures complete coverage of
the designated area, significantly improving operational efficiency and reliability. One notable challenge was the lack
of a native HDMI video output on the DJI Mavic Mini 2 SE, a common limitation among entry-level, cost-effective

3.4. LLM Integration for Actionable Insights
We aggregate the macro-level results, such as stressed area coordinates from YOLO and disease classifications
from DeiT, into a structured report. A fine-tuned LLM on agronomic literature interprets this report to produce farmer-
friendly recommendations. Whether advising on targeted fungicide treatments, suggesting irrigation adjustments, or
recommending crop rotations, the LLM ensures that technical findings translate into tangible improvements in farm
management.
4. Results and Discussion
4.1. Performance Evaluation
The following models are a comparison of performance to classify the stressed and healthy state of crops. Notable
improvements have been achieved by using advanced architectures and techniques.
• CNN Baseline (Accuracy: 86%) The convolutional neural network (CNN) provided a foundational bench-
mark. Though good for detecting general patterns, it lacks the ability to capture those subtle features indicative
of specific diseases.
• Standard Vision Transformer (ViT, Accuracy: 88%) With the use of global attention mechanisms, ViT is
able to outdo CNN by capturing more critical fine details. This really says it all about performance leverage
provided by transformer-based models in handling complex image data sets.
• Data-Efficient Vision Transformer (DeiT, Accuracy: 99.45%) With optimized hyperparameters and targeted
augmentation strategies, DeiT had the potential to reach near perfect accuracy, showing its efficiency in lever-
aging scarce data to perform much better than CNN and standard ViT baselines.
• YOLO (mAP: 95.4%, Precision: 93.2%, Recall: 91.8%) The YOLO architecture presented a very promising
result of object detection tasks, achieving a high mean average precision (mAP) of 95.4%. These results validate
that it’s highly effective for segregating crop conditions as either under stressed or healthy.
