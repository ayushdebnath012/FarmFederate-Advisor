Abstract— This study explains how a federated learning 
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model is used to diagnose 
the various degrees of beetroot leaf diseases. Beetroot crops are 
essential to the world's food systems, but several illnesses often 
affect their quality and productivity. To suggest a CNN model 
using federated learning to analyse data from several clients 
while preserving privacy for a quick and precise diagnosis. The 
method analyses the severity of the five stages of beetroot leaf 
diseases using a federated learning technique across several 
local clients to use precision, recall, F1-score, and accuracy as 
performance 
measurements. 
The 
review 
showed 
that 
performance had improved consistently for all customers. As an 
example, Client X had a prediction accuracy of 0.97, which 
increased gradually to 0.98 for both Client P and Client R. 
Additionally, as shown by the macro-average (94.15–94.17%), 
weighted average (94.67%), and micro-average (94.62%) scores 
for top-performing customers, the model's performance 
remained stable and resilient throughout all severity levels. The 
study's findings support using a CNN model built on federated 
learning for scalable, effective, and privacy-preserving disease 
control in agriculture. The promising results suggest the 
potential for extending such approaches to other agricultural 
products, considerably advancing efforts to ensure global food 
security. The research also showed that the CNN model could 
learn and develop continuously in a federated learning scenario, 
highlighting its usefulness as a dynamic tool that adapts to new 
illness patterns. A paradigm shift in the approach to combating 
agricultural illnesses and increasing food supply is heralded by 
this study, which reveals a potential junction between machine 
learning and agricultural disease management. The findings 
motivate more research into the model's adaptability to other 
crops and pests, enhancing its contribution to resilient and 
sustainable farming systems.

I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Due to its many uses in the culinary, medical, and industrial 
sectors, beetroot (Beta vulgaris) farming is a crucial 
agricultural activity worldwide, including India. The crop is 
vulnerable to a wide range of leaf diseases, which may reduce 
yield and harm farmers' ability to make a living. 
Understanding the severity of these illnesses in this context, 
particularly about the Indian subcontinent and internationally, 
becomes crucial [1]. The investigation focused on the five 
prevalent beetroot leaf diseases (Cercospora leaf spot, 
powdery mildew, Fusarium wilt, root-knot nematode, and beet 
curly top virus). Farmers have a great deal of difficulty 
because of these ailments, which range in intensity from 
moderate (1–20%) to severe (81–100%) and severely harm 
beetroot crops. The heterogeneity in these illnesses' degrees of 
severity was strongly correlated with the decline in crop 
output, with severe infections in extreme instances leading to 
the loss of practically all of the crop. Therefore, farmers must 
contend with both the severity of these illnesses and the 
vulnerability of their products to these diseases, adding 
another level of complication to an already tricky undertaking. 
Beetroot leaf diseases provide farmers with a variety of 
challenges. Many people need access to the necessary 
resources and information for proper illness detection and 
management [2]. 
Additionally, conventional diagnostic techniques depend 
significantly on knowledgeable agronomists, who may not be 
easily accessible in isolated agricultural areas. Even if a 
disease has been diagnosed, managing it calls for carefully 
selecting and using appropriate pesticides, which may be 
prohibitively expensive for smaller-scale farmers. The 
interaction of these elements often results in subpar illness 
management strategies, which worsen the situation. Despite 
these obstacles, Beetroot farming is a vital agricultural activity 
[3]. Beetroot is an essential crop for solving challenges with 
food security because of its high nutritional content and 
economic viability. The severity and constant danger of leaf 
diseases complicate this picture, highlighting the need for 
more efficient, widely available, and scalable management 
strategies for these disorders. Using convolutional neural

core of the study. The drawbacks of conventional techniques

C. Collaborative CNN Model for Federated Learning 
The CNN was used in the model architecture using a federated 
learning strategy. The need to scale while preserving data 
privacy and training a high-performing model was the driving 
force behind this. A max-pooling layer for feature extraction 
followed each convolutional layer in the CNN design. The 
max-pooling layers helped lower the output's spatial 
dimensions, hence reducing the processing requirements, 
while the convolutional layers used filters of various sizes to 
identify local characteristics in the pictures. These degrees of 
severity assist farmers and agronomists in precisely 
diagnosing the disease stage and implementing the necessary 
actions to manage the illness and minimize crop loss. It is 
essential to comprehend these levels to train the Federated 
Learning CNN model, which employs these categories to 
learn and forecast disease severity in beetroot crops. The 
characteristics were then interpreted, and predictions were 
created using fully linked layers. The last layer provided a 
probability distribution throughout the range of illness 
severity levels by using a softmax activation function. The use 
of federated learning is what makes the strategy unique. Each 
device (farm) in this scenario utilised its local data to calculate 
an update to the model parameters, and these changes were 
then combined at a central server to generate an overall model 
update. This procedure was repeated over several rounds until 
the model's performance matched the pre-established 
requirements in Figure 3.

assess the model's performance, considering the varied 
degrees of severity of beetroot leaf diseases. The macro-
