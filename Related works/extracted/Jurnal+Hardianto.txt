Accurately monitoring the health of house plants is becoming increasingly
important as public interest in urban farming and ornamental plants 
increases. This research develops a plant health prediction application 
using cell phone cameras and artificial intelligence (AI) based on
federated learning. This technology enables AI model training directly on 
the user's device without having to send image data to a central server, 
thus maintaining user privacy and network efficiency. The app uses plant 
image classification models to detect disease symptoms such as leaf spot,
wilting, and discoloration. Data is collected from various users, and the 
model is trained in a decentralized manner through a federated averaging
protocol. Test results show that the system is able to achieve prediction
accuracy above 85% in detecting plant conditions, with low latency and 
minimal data consumption. This approach shows significant potential in 
the application of secure, distributed, and user-friendly AI for home plant
monitoring.

1. 
INTRODUCTION 
In recent years, public interest in ornamental plants and household plants has increased significantly 
(Andri & Tumbuan, 2015). This phenomenon not only occurs in urban areas as a form of spatial 
aesthetics, but also as part of a healthy and environmentally friendly lifestyle. House plants such as 
monstera, betel nut, cactus, and various varieties of succulents are now an important part of interior 
decoration as well as homeowners' activities in maintaining air quality and reducing stress. However, 
not all plant owners have sufficient knowledge on how to optimally care for their plants, especially in 
recognizing the early signs of plant stress, pest attack, or disease (Kampus & Dramaga, n.d.).  
With advances in mobile device technology, almost everyone now has access to high-resolution 
cameras and internet connectivity. This opens up opportunities to develop visual and artificial 
intelligence-based applications that can be used directly on the user's device, without the need for 
additional tools or high costs. The utilization of mobile phone cameras as visual monitoring tools allows 
the process of identifying disease symptoms in plants to be done quickly, practically, and without special 
skills. However, processing large amounts of plant image data raises concerns about privacy, data 
security, and computational efficiency. The Federated Learning approach is a strategic solution because 
it enables collaborative AI model training among user devices without the need to send raw data to a 
central server. With FL, AI models can be continuously improved in accuracy by utilizing the collective 
experience of many users, without compromising the privacy of their personal data. 
Leaf discoloration, spotting, or unnatural growth are often ignored until they cause permanent 
damage. Therefore, a support system is needed that is able to assist home users in monitoring plant 
health conditions quickly, easily, and accurately (Latifah et al., 2021). Plant health is an important factor 
in the success of cultivation, both in the large-scale agricultural sector as well as in household-scale

The development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology offers innovative solutions in 
detecting and predicting plant health conditions. By utilizing the camera on smartphones, users can now 
take pictures of plants and get an automated analysis of their condition. However, behind this 
convenience, there are new challenges related to data privacy and limited computing infrastructure on 
user devices. Federated Learning (FL) emerges as a smart approach to overcome these challenges. 
Unlike conventional AI approaches that require data collection to a central server, FL allows AI model 
training to be done in a distributed manner across user devices. Thus, the user's personal data, including 
plant images and environmental metadata, remains secure and does not have to leave the device. 
The health of houseplants is an increasing concern in modern urban life, where plants not only 
serve as decorative elements, but also provide psychological benefits, improve air quality, and create a 
healthier living environment. However, many house plant owners do not have sufficient knowledge or 
skills in early detection of disease or stress in plants. In this context, artificial intelligence (AI) technology 
based on computer vision through smart phone cameras can be a practical and affordable solution in 
detecting plant health automatically and in real-time. AI-based crop health prediction applications have 
actually developed rapidly in recent years, but most of the research focuses on large-scale agricultural 
crops and uses centralized datasets (Mahendra et al., 2024). This approach is not fully suited to the 
household context, which has more varied crop types, uncontrolled shooting environments, and user 
privacy limitations. In addition, most conventional machine learning models require data to be collected 
and sent to a central server, which potentially raises privacy and security concerns for user data 
(Sudirwo et al., 2025). 
Federated Learning (FL) comes as a new approach in training AI models without having to send 
raw data to a central server. In this context, models are trained locally on the user's device (e.g., mobile 
phone), and only model parameters are shared with the central server for combination. This approach 
offers advantages in terms of privacy protection and communication efficiency. Therefore, the 
application of FL in HP camera-based house plant health prediction applications is a very relevant and 
significant innovation (Besar, n.d.).  
This research is important because it fills a gap in the literature that is still minimal regarding 
the application of Federated Learning for plant health diagnosis in the household environment. Most 
previous studies have not specifically integrated FL technology with cell phone cameras in the context 
of house plants, which have very diverse visual and environmental characteristics. In addition, this 
research also responds to the need for a solution that is inclusive, personalized, and practical for non-
expert users in detecting plant diseases.  The main focus of this research is on the development of a home 
plant health prediction application that can be accessed through a mobile phone camera by utilizing AI 
technology based on Federated Learning (Nirsal et al., 2025). This application will be able to detect the 
early symptoms of plant diseases based on images taken with a cell phone camera. 
This research has three main contributions: (1) developing a prototype AI-based mobile 
application capable of predicting the health conditions of house plants using a cell phone camera, (2) 
implementing a Federated Learning architecture to maintain user data privacy and improve system 
efficiency, and (3) evaluating system performance in real-world scenarios with variations in plant types 
and lighting conditions.

RESEARCH METHOD 
This research belongs to the type of applied research that aims to present concrete solutions to real 
problems faced by home users in caring for plants, especially in terms of early detection of plant diseases. 
By utilizing highly accessible mobile phone camera technology combined with artificial intelligence (AI) 
and Federated Learning (FL) approaches, this research offers relevant and applicable innovations 
amidst the increasing public interest in home farming and urban farming. The main target population is 
home gardeners who use smartphone devices to care for and monitor the condition of their plants. This 
population includes individuals of various ages, professions, and levels of horticultural knowledge in 
both urban and suburban environments who have an interest in small-scale ornamental, medicinal, or 
food crops grown in yards, balconies, or indoors. This population was selected based on its main 
characteristic, the use of smartphones as the main device for plant documentation and monitoring. This 
is in line with the research objective of developing a home plant disease detection application based on 
cell phone cameras and AI, so users from this group are representative of the actual usage scenario. Data 
collection in this study was conducted through a combined approach of technology-based direct 
observation, digital documentation, and user questionnaires, with the main focus on capturing images 
of plants through a cell phone camera as well as user interaction with the developed application. These 
techniques were designed to generate two main types of data: visual image data of plants and 
behavioral/interaction data of users towards the system.  The main technique for data collection was 
image capturing done directly by respondents using their mobile phone cameras.  In this study, the data 
analysis techniques were quantitative and experimental, focusing on processing the plant image data, 
evaluating the performance of the AI model, and analyzing the effectiveness of the Federated Learning 
(FL) approach over the centralized approach. Data analysis was divided into three main components: AI 
model performance analysis, FL system efficiency analysis, and user feedback analysis.  
3. 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS 
This section presents the results obtained from the development and evaluation of the health prediction 
application for house plants, which utilizes smartphone cameras and artificial intelligence (AI) 
integrated with Federated Learning (FL). The discussion highlights system performance, prediction 
accuracy, privacy preservation, and computational efficiency, followed by a comparative analysis with 
centralized learning approaches. 
3.1. 
Model and Dataset Implementation 
The machine learning model used is a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), which is optimized for 
mobile devices using the MobileNetV2 architecture. The dataset used consists of 15,000 images of house 
plants, obtained through the voluntary participation of 200 smartphone users over 2 months. Each 
image was categorized into 6 classes, namely: 
a. 
Healthy 
b. 
Fungal infection 
c. 
Bacterial spot 
d. 
Pest infestation 
e. 
Nutrient deficiency 
f. 
Overwatering 
The dataset is divided into 80% training data and 20% testing data, with variations in device, camera 
resolution, and lighting conditions that represent real field conditions. 
 
The implementation of the health prediction model for house plants revolves around leveraging 
deep learning techniques, specifically convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to perform image 
classification and diagnosis of plant health status. CNNs were chosen due to their strong performance in 
image recognition tasks and their ability to extract hierarchical features from plant images, such as leaf 
color, texture, and shape-which are critical indicators of plant health. The architecture used in this 
research is based on a modified MobileNetV2 model, chosen for its balance between accuracy and 
computational efficiency. MobileNetV2 is lightweight and optimized for mobile and embedded devices, 
making it suitable for deployment in a smartphone-based application. The model consists of depthwise 
separable convolutions and inverted residual blocks, which significantly reduce the number of 
parameters while maintaining high accuracy. 
 
The model outputs a multi-class classification, where each class corresponds to a specific plant 
health condition (e.g., healthy, nutrient deficiency, fungal infection, pest damage, etc.). A softmax

activation function is applied in the output layer to provide a probability distribution across classes. The 
training objective uses a categorical cross-entropy loss function, optimized via the Adam optimizer with 
a learning rate scheduler to enhance convergence. To support privacy-preserving learning, the model 
was implemented within a Federated Learning (FL) framework using TensorFlow Federated (TFF). This 
setup allows the model to be trained across multiple devices (clients) without transferring raw image 
data to a central server. Instead, each device computes model updates locally, which are then aggregated 
centrally using the Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm. This decentralized learning process 
reduces privacy risks and bandwidth usage while enabling continuous model improvement. Security 
and efficiency in the FL setup were further reinforced through differential privacy mechanisms and 
model compression techniques. Differential privacy adds noise to the local updates to obscure specific 
user data, while model quantization ensures the FL communication overhead remains minimal. 
 
The development of an effective and reliable model necessitates a diverse and well-annotated 
dataset. Given the scarcity of publicly available datasets tailored specifically for house plants, the 
research team constructed a custom dataset by collecting over 15,000 labeled images of common house 
plants under varying conditions. Images were gathered through crowd-sourcing, direct photography, 
and augmented with publicly available datasets such as PlantVillage and AI Challenger Crop Disease 
Dataset, after ensuring compliance with licensing terms and adjusting labels to suit the research scope. 
The implementation of the plant health prediction model using CNNs within a Federated Learning 
framework has demonstrated promising results in terms of performance, scalability, and user privacy. 
The use of MobileNetV2 allows real-time analysis on mobile devices, while FL ensures that users’ image 
data remains secure. The custom dataset, tailored to the home environment and enriched with real-
world diversity, plays a critical role in model robustness. Despite challenges related to data 
heterogeneity and system limitations, the application sets a strong foundation for scalable, privacy-
preserving AI in smart home agriculture.

3.3. 
Field Trial and User Feedback 
After the training was completed, the app was tested by 50 users for 14 days. They were asked to take 
pictures of their plants and receive diagnosis results from the app. 
a. 
User survey results 
b. 
Satisfaction level: 86% satisfied with the diagnosis results 
c. 
Average response time: 2.3 seconds per prediction 
d. 
Perceived accuracy: 82% felt the diagnosis matched reality 
e. 
Trust in privacy: 91% felt safer using the system without image uploads 
This trial shows that the system works well in real-life conditions, even on mid-range phones. 
To evaluate the real-world performance and usability of the developed mobile application, a field trial 
was conducted involving 50 voluntary participants over a 14-day period. Participants were selected to 
represent a broad demographic, including plant enthusiasts, casual users, and urban farmers. Each 
participant installed the application on their personal Android smartphone, used the app to capture 
images of their house plants, and received instant feedback regarding the plant’s health condition. 
The primary objectives of the trial were to assess: 
a. 
Diagnostic accuracy from the user's perspective 
b. 
Application responsiveness and performance under diverse device and environmental conditions 
c. 
User experience (UX) and satisfaction 
d. 
Perceived trust and confidence in data privacy and security 
All user interactions with the app were logged (with user consent), and a post-trial survey was 
administered to collect qualitative and quantitative feedback. 
During the trial, the application was used to analyze a total of 2,436 plant images across different lighting 
conditions, camera types, and plant species. The following observations were recorded: Average 
response time: 2.3 seconds per prediction (measured from image capture to displaying results) Failure 
rate: 3.7%, mostly due to poor image quality (e.g., blur or insufficient lighting), Battery consumption: 
Moderate (an estimated 4–6% per 10 image evaluations) Model synchronization (federated updates): 
Successfully completed by 87% of users during the trial period,These metrics confirm the system's 
ability to operate efficiently on a wide range of smartphones, maintaining reasonable computational and 
energy demands.
