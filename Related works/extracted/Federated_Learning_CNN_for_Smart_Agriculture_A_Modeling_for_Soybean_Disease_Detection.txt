Abstract— With the help of federated learning and CNNs, this 
research paper aims to identify and categorize soybean leaf 
diseases. This study uses a federated learning approach to 
classify disease severity into four classes using four clients. The 
benefit of this method is that it preserves data privacy during 
analytics, which is highly important in our digital age. The 
information we obtain from each local customer is used to 
conduct our analysis. Using federated averaging, we produce an 
aggregate global model. It is possible to assess the model's 
performance on a local and international level by calculating 
macro, micro, and weighted averages. As a result of the macro 
standard stressing class equality, clients yu_1 through yu_6 
performed well with grades of 91.42%, 91.08%, 90.92%, 
93.15%, and 93.40. Based on the weighted average of those 
clients with class imbalance, 91.43%, 91.07%, 90.92%, 93.13%, 
93.41%, and 92.86%, respectively. Finally, the micro average 
averaging all inputs to the model indicates its overall accuracy 
with the following percentages for each client: 91.43%, 91.06%, 
90.91%, and 93.11%.Using federated averaging to transform 
local data into a global data model proved this approach feasible 
in a real-world scenario. Precision, recall, and F1-scores for the 
studied clients indicate high sensitivity and specificity of the 
model. A precise agriculture technique can be adapted to 
control soybean foliar disorders resulting from this study. As a 
part of our move towards more resilient and safe farm 
management approaches, this study helps us solve the urgent 
task of detecting and classifying diseases.

C. Federated Learning CNN Model 
A client network is set up as follows: The dataset is divided 
into six networks, each including parts of the dataset. 
Considering that these customers come from worldwide, we 
quickly gather information from various angles to serve them 
better. 
Our process is based on federated learning. This involves 
building a model for each client based on their data to achieve 
our goal of federated education. The decentralization of the 
training ensures data protection while taking advantage of 
existing computational facilities close to the participant's 
location and ensuring data privacy. 
There is the model averaging concept, in which the model 
parameters are averaged over each client after training the 
model. As a result of using this approach, a model learns from 
all available datasets without requiring explicit data sharing 
between them as part of the iterative average procedure.
