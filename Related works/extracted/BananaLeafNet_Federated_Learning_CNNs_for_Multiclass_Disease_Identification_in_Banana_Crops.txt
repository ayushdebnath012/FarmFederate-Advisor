Abstract— This research study explores a unique use of 
convolutional neural networks (CNN) in federated learning 
(FL) for the classification of banana leaf diseases across a 
decentralised network of five clients (pm_1 to pm_5), each of 
which represents a different agricultural situation. The goal of 
the research, which covers five classes of banana leaf illnesses 
(UT-1 to UT-5), is to provide a reliable and private-protecting 
remedy for the many problems encountered in banana 
farming. By using FL's decentralised training paradigm, every 
client independently improves its model using data that is 
stored locally, guaranteeing the privacy of user information. 
The effectiveness of the model at the local level is shown in the 
Result Analysis section, where precise values range from 
87.45% to 92.17%, recall values from 87.61% to 92.20%, and 
F1-Score values from 87.41% to 92.16%. Then, by examining 
Federated Averaging, the standardised model performance is 
shown using macro, micro, and weighted averages. The macro 
average accuracy, recall, and F1-Score of 87.49%, 87.58%, and 
90.02%, respectively, show that the customers' performance is 
balanced. Weighted averages that account for class imbalances 
produce yield percentages of 87.43%, 87.53%, and 90.00%, in 
that order. Micro Averages with accuracy, recall, and F1-
Scores of 87.39%, 87.50%, and 89.97% are obtained by 
summing true positives, false positives, and false negatives 
among customers. The investigation into using Federated 
Averaging to transform local data into global data highlights 
the model's ability to work collaboratively across individual 
client performances. This transforming process explains a 
coherent and strong global model performance, as shown by 
the macro, micro, and weighted averages. This study highlights 
the potential of FL with CNN to solve issues in precision 
agriculture while also advancing the knowledge of disease 
categorization via the integration of technical innovation and 
agricultural imperatives.

The methodology portion of this study explains in great 
depth how the research was conducted. To take on the 
problem of illness categorization in banana leaves by 
combining Federated Learning (FL) with Convolutional 
Neural Networks (CNN). The detailed stages, along with an

A. Data Scrubbing, Procurement, and Amplification: 
Given the variety of banana leaf diseases, comprehensive 
data collection is essential. To ensure that the dataset 
included the varied presentations of these illnesses, to 
gathered data from five unique customers for each of the five 
disease classifications. Collaborated with customers to 
collect photographs of banana leaves at varying levels of 
disease severity to use as data sources.Labelling: Assisted 
domain specialists in categorizing photos into illness 
groups.Data Augmentation: used methods like rotation, 
zooming, and flipping to artificially increase the dataset and 
guarantee that the CNN is resilient to fluctuations, as shown 
in Figure 2.

B. Federated_Learning_CNN_Model 
To facilitate CNN convergence, normalize pictures by 
transforming their pixel values to a uniform range, generally 
[0, 1]. The Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) forms the 
foundation of the approach because of its proven success in 
picture categorization. Convolutional layers, pooling layers, 
and fully linked layers were included in the model's layer 
design. Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation functions 
were used for the hidden layers, while softmax activation 
functions were used for the output layer, guaranteeing a 
uniform 
distribution 
of 
illness 
classes.Dropout 
and 
Regularisation: Dropout layers were included to reduce the 
likelihood of overfitting, while L2 regularisation was used to 
penalize too complicated models.Using the FL paradigm, to 
could implement decentralized training without requiring the 
exchange of raw data. Each of the 25 clients (5 for each 
condition) used the global model's weights to initialize their 
local CNN model. Customers trained their models using data 
available to them locally. Clients aggregated their model 
weights by sending them to a centralized server when local 
training was complete. Weighted averaging of the models 
was carried out by the centralized server in order to update 
the global model. The original contribution is improving the 
averaging method that guarantees more stable global model 
updates. Client Contribution Evaluation: Assessed the 
accuracy of each client's changes by measuring how well 
they were validated.By assigning clients different weights 
depending on the value of their input, to can ensure that more 
informative updates have a more significant impact on the 
overall model, as illustrated in Table 1.

layer. The input to the model is a 354x354, three-channel 
(likely RGB) picture. These layers, known as convolutional 
layers, are crucial for feature extraction. Feature maps are 
generated when filters iteratively pass over the input picture. 
My filters are 3x3, and the depth (the number of filters) 
increases with each successive layer. Spatial down-sampling 
is achieved with the use of Max-Pooling Layers. Here, I've 
assumed a pooling size of 2x2, halving the feature map's 
dimensions on every iteration. Decisions are made by fully 
connected layers, which receive the characteristics retrieved 
by convolutional layers. There are 12 types of banana leaf 
illnesses, hence 12 neurons in the last layer. In light of the 
current model and data, If you have 2636 photos and want to 
feed them into the model in batches, you may adjust the 
batch size to match the capacity of your memory. If you
