1. Introduction
Data science techniques have been used for plant leaf 
disease detection and management, and models have been 
developed for low-cost, efficient, and environmentally 
sustainable solutions to improve crop productivity (Kaur et 
al., 2022; Khullar et al., 2025). Precision agriculture relying 
on modern technologies improves crop productivity. 
Various revolutionized modern terminologies have been 
used under precision agriculture to enhance productivity 
(Geetharamani and Arun Pandian, 2019). Better disease 
detection solutions reduce the dependency on pesticides 
and plant protection products (Li et al., 2021; Ouhami et 
al., 2021, Antico et al., 2024). This ultimately impacts the 
economy, crop quality, and yield. In recent years, various 
technologies have been used to develop sustainable 
solutions for plant disease detection (Tugrul et al., 2022). 
Deep neural networks are widely used for resolving 
agriculture issues (Borhani et al., 2022). Despite these 
techniques yielding excellent results and surpassing 
expectations, they encounter challenges concerning data 
security due to the storage of data on a centralized server. 
Thus, additional development is needed to address security 
concerns for centrally stored data.
Federated learning (FL) is the most promising 
approach to resolve issues with centralized approaches. 
Centrally stored data is used to train artificial intelligence 
models and uncover valuable information on production 
techniques (Maillet et al., 2023) and disease management. 
However, accessing this data brings with it the risk of 
data breaches. FL enables entities to collaborate or work 
together without sharing their private data and maintain 
confidentiality and data security. FL empowers the 
model both remotely and locally. Moreover, it minimizes 
data sharing and provides full data control to the user 
and minimizes data leakage (Kerkech et al., 2020). FL 
ensures privacy policy adherence and alleviates resource 
utilization. All the participants in FL benefit from the 
model trained on quality data without the security 
risks (Wen et al., 2023). Although FL is a very effective

Abstract: The detection and management of horticultural crop diseases is crucial for enhancing productivity, reducing costs, and 
promoting environmentally sustainable practices. Recently, various techniques have been developed for managing crop-related issues, 
including leaf diseases and pest control. A centralized control approach has been implemented for disease detection. This centralized 
system faced significant challenges, including concerns over data privacy and the costs associated with data migration. This study 
addressed the problems with centralized solutions through a federated learning approach enhanced by the attention mechanism of the 
vision transformer (ViT). We investigated the key features of the federated learning method and propose a deep learning model based 
on federated learning, utilizing the attention mechanism of the ViT to detect diseases in mango plant leaves. The study utilized an open-
access dataset of mango leaf diseases sourced from Kaggle. The performance of the federated model depended heavily on several factors, 
including data quality, the number of participating agents, their communication efficiency, and the number of local iterations. Although 
various pretrained models were tested to address these challenges, the lightweight ViT was the most effective, achieving an impressive 
accuracy rate of 98.99% in federated learning scenarios.

2. Related work
This section summarizes centralized techniques for leaf 
disease detection. Several state-of-the-art models are 
available that process data jointly or individually. 
Despite the increasing interest in the implementation 
of FL across various fields, its utilization in the agricultural 
industry, especially for the detection of plant leaf disease, 
remains limited. A review of the current literature 
indicates that only a handful of studies have addressed 
important issues such as data privacy, security, and disease 
detection in agriculture. Many challenges have not been 
addressed, including the analysis of disease severity, weed 
detection, soil fertility assessment, and pest identification 
(Abdulridha et al., 2016; Ganatra and Patel, 2020; Xu et 
al., 2021).
This paper by Chimate et al. (2025) examines effective 
feature extraction methods designed to enhance the 
accuracy of disease classification in mango and groundnut 
crops. By refining feature extraction techniques, the study 
improved the performance of plant disease detection 
systems, which could lead to better management of crop 
health. They emphasize advanced feature extraction 
strategies to bolster disease identification processes in 
agricultural engineering. The paper describes a method 
for detecting pests that affect mangoes through advanced 
statistical 
and 
machine 
learning 
techniques—the 
Gaussian mixture model and expectation-maximization 
algorithm—for more accurate detection (Pansy, 2025). 
Swapno et al. (2024) developed a CNN and generated 
encouraging results, with accuracy rates between 93.12% 
and 96.67% in identifying various mango leaf diseases. The 
innovative deep learning framework utilizes ensemble and 
transfer learning techniques to accurately identify mango 
leaf diseases, achieving an impressive 94.99% testing 
accuracy. 
Neog and Kaur (2025) integrated multiple neural 
network models (ResNet50, VGG19, MobileNet, and 
a custom CNN) to improve detection efficiency and 
robustness in mango leaf disease classification, exceeding 
the performance of individual models in critical metrics 
like precision and recall. The system uses a comprehensive 
dataset of 8000 images, data augmentation, and advanced 
training strategies to enable early disease diagnosis, 
thus supporting sustainable mango cultivation and crop 
management (Neog and Kaur, 2025). The study by Varma 
et al. (2025) investigated the application of various transfer 
learning models to automatically identify diseases in 
mango leaves, highlighting the significance of early and 
precise diagnosis for effective crop management. The 
research by Varma et al. (2025) introduces a deep learning 
methodology that uses the EfficientNetB5 model to 
effectively categorize mango leaf images into eight distinct 
groups, encompassing six types of diseases, healthy leaves, 
and dieback, achieving a remarkable test accuracy of 97%.
Aluvala and Chauhan (2025) suggested a framework 
that serves as a promising tool for accurate disease 
diagnosis, contributing to sustainable agriculture, food 
security, and economic growth, with the potential for real-
time application in agricultural practices. They outline 
an innovative strategy that merges deep learning with 
ensemble learning techniques to enhance the diagnosis 
of mango leaf diseases. By utilizing advanced machine 
learning models, the proposed approaches seeks to 
improve accuracy and reliability in disease detection, 
which is essential for timely intervention and effective 
crop management. These comprehensive methods address 
the shortcomings of conventional diagnostic techniques 
and provide a valuable resource for agricultural decision-
making (Gautam et al., 2024; Gautam et al., 2025).
This article introduced a groundbreaking method for 
detecting mango diseases by integrating a fused ViT with 
ConvNeXt architecture, with the goal of improving accuracy 
and robustness in disease identification. Combining 
sophisticated deep learning models to analyze intricate 
visual patterns in mango leaf images ultimately promotes 
better crop health management (Alamri et al., 2025). Alamri 
et al. (2025) investigated the impact of preprocessing 
techniques on classification accuracy using five CNN 
models, both prior to and following data preprocessing. 
The results indicate significant improvements in accuracy

In centralized machine learning systems, all client-side 
data must be sent to a central server for model training 
and inference. Although centralized methods—such as 
those described by Zhong and Zhao (2020) and Mac et 
al. (2024)—perform well when utilizing architectures like 
DenseNet121 and VGG19, they raise concerns in real-world 
agriculture. Farmers, agritech companies, and cooperatives 
frequently function in data-sensitive environments, where 
insights regarding crop conditions, disease trends, and 
yield predictions are viewed as confidential or proprietary. 
Research indicates the reluctance of stakeholders to share 
unprocessed agricultural data due to concerns over privacy 
violations, market manipulation, and data exploitation.
Moreover, remote farm locations with limited data 
connectivity increases the cost and latency challenges with 
large-scale data transfer, rendering centralized methods 
impractical on a larger scale. These constraints have 
propelled recent investigations—such as those conducted 
by Vats et al. (2024), Singh et al. (2024), and Hari and Singh 
(2025)—toward FL. FL is a decentralized framework that 
facilitates collaborative model training without the need 
for raw data exchange. The growing use of FL signifies a 
paradigm shift aimed at achieving both model precision 
and data sovereignty in precision agriculture (Table 1).

Among the latest methods for detecting plant diseases, 
ViTs have shown great potential in centralized systems, 
owing to their ability to model global relationships through 
self-attention mechanisms. In addition to enhancing 
accuracy, the selection of ViT over traditional CNN-
based architectures was influenced by its representational 
benefits and computational considerations. Unlike CNNs 
that depend on localized receptive fields, ViTs utilize self-
attention to capture long-range dependencies. This is 
particularly advantageous for detecting mango leaf diseases 
where lesions tend to be small, dispersed, and affected by 
the overall context. This improves generalization across 
intricate backgrounds.
From a computational standpoint, ViTs generally 
necessitate larger training datasets and incur greater 
memory costs due to the quadratic complexity of 
attention. These challenges have been addressed through 
dataset 
augmentation, 
meticulous 
hyperparameter 
optimization, and GPU-accelerated training, ensuring 
operational efficiency. When compared to deeper 
CNNs such as EfficientNet-B7, ViTs provided a superior 
equilibrium between accuracy and inference speed, as 
the self-attention mechanism minimizes redundant 
convolutions. Furthermore, ViTs are inherently scalable; 
their patch-based input allows for better adaptability to 
high-resolution images without a proportional increase in 
computation, unlike CNNs that scale quadratically with 
resolution.
Therefore, the use of ViT is warranted not only because 
of its accuracy but also its capacity to efficiently capture 
global dependencies, scalability to larger input resolutions, 
and competitive inference performance in comparison to 
very deep CNNs.
Consequently, the current study suggests the 
incorporation of ViTs into an FL framework to improve 
the accuracy, generalization, and privacy of systems 
designed for detecting plant leaf diseases. By utilizing the 
attention mechanisms of ViTs within FL, the proposed 
strategy seeks to:
•	
Enhance classification performance across diverse 
client data.
•	
Safeguard data privacy without compromising model 
quality.
•	
Facilitate scalable implementation in resource-limited 
agricultural contexts.
The primary focus of this study was to characterize 
diseases in mango plant leaves, while also examining the 
impact of:
•	
Device heterogeneity (variations in client devices).
•	
The number of local iterations per device during 
federated training.
The role and effectiveness of the ViT attention 
mechanism in enhancing detection accuracy was assessed. 
The contributions of this research are listed here:
1.	 Explore the impact of attention mechanism for 
characterized plant leaf disease using FL.
2.	 Study the impact of varying devices and iteration 
within the devices in the experiment of leaf disease 
detection.
3.	 Explored the advantage of FL in plant leaf disease 
detection.
3. Materials and methods
This section of the paper covers the details of the dataset 
used and the methodology applied to characterize leaf 
disease.
3.1. Dataset
To address the issues of nonindependent and identically 
distributed (non-IID) data and class imbalance in FL, 
particularly in the context of agricultural disease detection, 
researchers have put forward various strategies. This study 
used a dataset with eight classes, sourced from a prominent 
open-source repository, to train local clients within an 
FL framework. The dataset contains images of leaves 
afflicted by seven prevalent diseases—powdery mildew, 
anthracnose, dieback, bacterial canker, sooty mould, gall 
midge, and cutting weevil—alongside images of healthy 
leaves. Comprehensive statistics regarding the dataset 
are provided in Table 2, while representative samples are 
illustrated in Figure 1.
Although the current study is based on a balanced 
dataset, it is crucial to recognize that actual agricultural 
data is often imbalanced. Typically, only a limited 
number of samples represent rare diseases, while more 
common diseases are disproportionately represented. This 
imbalance presents several challenges within federated 
environments. In particular, clients may only have data 
related to a restricted subset of classes, leading to non-
IID data distributions. Such heterogeneity in data poses a 
significant challenge in FL, as it can result in biased local 
updates, model bias, and a decrease in the speed of global 
convergence.
Various operations such augmentation and data 
sampling were carried out to address the challenges of 
non-IID data and class imbalance in FL.
4. Attention mechanism-based deep neural network for 
FL
In a decentralized approach as FL the multiple clients train 
their local model and collaborate to build a global model 
(Li et al., 2020; Hassan and Maji, 2022). Figure 2 illustrates 
the proposed methodology where the local machines are 
trained with a publicly available mango leaf disease dataset 
and afterward collaborate to build or improve a global 
model. In the network, ViT was utilized to classify plant 
leaf images. The following section describes the complete 
FL procedure.
